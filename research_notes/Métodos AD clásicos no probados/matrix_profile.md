# Matrix Profile / Time-Series Discord Discovery for Multivariate, Contaminated, Online Telemetry Anomaly Detection

## Is there a well-established multivariate extension (mSTAMP / per-channel-then-fuse) and what is the 2023–2026 state of the art?

### Takeaway
Yes — multivariate matrix profile (MP) is well established, with two competing families of extension: the original **mSTAMP** "joint k-dimensional" formulation (Yeh, Kavantzas & Keogh, ICDM 2017) and simpler **per-channel-then-fuse** approaches (compute a 1-D MP per channel independently, then sum/sort/max across channels). The most recent and most rigorous treatment (Yeh et al., 2024/2026) is a systematic empirical study of fusion strategies that argues per-channel-then-fuse ("post-sorting"/"pre-sorting") is both simpler and, in a large benchmark, competitive with or better than deep learning baselines. This is a genuinely active, still-moving research area (patents, AAAI 2026, and TSB-AD 2026 submissions all appeared recently).

### Cited Findings
- **Matrix Profile VI: Meaningful Multidimensional Motif Discovery**, Chin-Chia Michael Yeh, Nickolas Kavantzas, Eamonn Keogh, ICDM 2017, pp. 565–574 — introduces **mSTAMP**, extending the univariate STAMP algorithm with a k-dimensional distance function to find motifs that are only meaningful across a subset of dimensions (not all of them); argues that naively aggregating all dimensions produces meaningless motifs except in contrived cases. — [PDF](https://www.cs.ucr.edu/~eamonn/Motif_Discovery_ICDM.pdf), [project page](https://sites.google.com/view/mstamp)
- **"Matrix Profile for Anomaly Detection on Multidimensional Time Series"**, Chin-Chia Michael Yeh, Audrey Der, Uday Singh Saini, Vivian Lai, Yan Zheng, Junpeng Wang, Xin Dai, Zhongfang Zhuang, Yujie Fan, Huiyuan Chen, Prince Osei Aboagye, Liang Wang, Wei Zhang, Eamonn Keogh, arXiv:2409.09298 (2024, v2) — systematically compares strategies for condensing the n×n×d pairwise-distance tensor into a 1-D anomaly score: **post-sorting** (find nearest neighbor per dimension independently, then sort dimensions at each time step), **pre-sorting** (sort distances across dimensions per subsequence pair before finding neighbors; adapted from mSTAMP's motif method but reversed/without cumulative sum), and cheaper **post-max/pre-max** variants. Benchmarks against 19 baselines (including deep models Telemanom/LSTM, OmniAnomaly/stochastic RNN, DeepAnT/CNN, Torsk/echo-state network) on 119 multidimensional datasets (unsupervised/supervised/semi-supervised). Headline claim: "MP is the only method that consistently delivers high performance across all setups." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09298)
- Same paper, reported scores (mean ± std) for the pre-sorting MP variant: unsupervised AUC-ROC 0.757±0.240, AUC-PR 0.324±0.321; semi-supervised AUC-ROC 0.717±0.226, AUC-PR 0.252±0.334. MP loses to Telemanom in semi-supervised and to kMeans in unsupervised settings, but differences are not statistically significant (paired t-test, α=0.05). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09298)
- Dataset scale in that benchmark: 119 datasets (48 unsupervised, 25 supervised, 46 semi-supervised) from 8 collections, dimensionality 2–38 channels, 8,849,589 total time steps (≈74,000 points/dataset on average, ~8.1 dims weighted average). Full benchmark (19 methods × 119 datasets) took ~2h22m with 16 parallel processes on an AMD EPYC 7513 server CPU. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09298)
- A 2026 technical report by Yeh describes an "MMPAD" submission to the **TSB-AD** benchmark combining pre-sorted multidimensional aggregation, exclusion-zone-aware k-NN retrieval for repeated anomalies, and moving-average post-processing, with an open-source implementation linked. Numeric leaderboard results were not retrievable from the fetched content. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.02445)
- **dtaianomaly** (open-source Python anomaly detection library) ships a `MatrixProfileDetector` that runs STOMP and, for multivariate input, computes the profile **per dimension separately and sums them** — i.e., the simple post-sorting/sum fusion strategy, not joint mSTAMP distance. — [dtaianomaly docs](https://dtaianomaly.readthedocs.io/en/0.4.0/_modules/dtaianomaly/anomaly_detection/MatrixProfileDetector.html)
- **STUMPY**'s `stumpy.mstump` (and distributed `mstumped` for Dask) implements multi-dimensional MP per the Yeh/Kavantzas/Keogh 2017 formulation (STOMP-based), taking input shaped (d, n) — rows are dimensions, columns are time. — [STUMPY docs](https://stumpy.readthedocs.io/en/stable/_modules/stumpy/mstumped.html)
- A 2025 Chinese aerospace journal paper, "A Multi-dimensional Telemetry Data Pattern Mining Method Based on Matrix Profile" (Le Lou, Zhen Liu, *Telecommunication Engineering*), targets satellite status monitoring using a multivariate MP method, but is offline/batch processing of 3-D telemetry data — does not establish real-time/online operation, and full method details were not retrievable. — [journal page](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/dxjs/EN/1251226695886385719)
- A real spacecraft application with a **concrete, genuinely novel multivariate MP extension**: Daigavane, Wagstaff, Doran, Cochrane, Jackman & Rymer, **"Detection of Environment Transitions in Time Series Data for Responsive Science"**, MileTS '20 (6th KDD Workshop on Mining and Learning from Time Series), Aug 24 2020 — proposes a "Multidimensional Matrix Profile" (MMP) defined as the **sum across dimensions of independently-computed per-dimension 1-D matrix profiles** (Eq. 6: MMP_i = Σ_d MP^(d)_i), explicitly contrasted with mSTAMP's k-smallest-values formulation. States computational complexity explicitly: **O(n²) per dimension, O(n²d) total**, plus O(nd) to sum across dimensions. — [PDF](https://www.wkiri.com/research/papers/daigavane-transitions-MiLeTS-20.pdf)
- A 2026 AAAI paper notes mSTAMP-style extensions "can be slower than vanilla STAMP at some dimension/length combinations" and that "existing extensions still struggle with complex multivariate temporal structure." — [AAAI PDF](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/38550/42512)

### Inferences
- There is no single canonical "best" multivariate MP method; the field has converged on **per-channel MP + a cheap fusion rule (sum, sort, or max)** as the pragmatic default, rather than mSTAMP's more expensive joint k-dimensional distance. This is good news for implementability: per-channel 1-D matrix profile (univariate STOMP, well supported by mature libraries) plus a simple fusion step is both the simplest and the empirically best-validated approach in the most recent large-scale study.
- Given the project's benchmark already includes PCA→KMeans→LOF and several autoencoders, a matrix-profile baseline using post-sorting/sum fusion would be a genuinely different detection paradigm (nearest-neighbor/distance-based discord discovery rather than reconstruction-error or density-based), which argues it is a legitimate "classical AD method not yet tried" for the related-work/ablation section.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the full numeric results table (Table IV) from the 2024 Yeh et al. paper comparing MP against all 19 baselines individually — only the aggregate claim and the MP-specific scores were extracted.
- No paper found that applies matrix profile specifically to the ESA-ADB dataset or a directly comparable 47-channel/18-second-cadence spacecraft telemetry benchmark.

## Is Matrix Profile usable in a genuinely online/incremental setting, or does it require fixed-window batch recompute?

### Takeaway
A true incremental/streaming algorithm exists for **univariate** series (STOMPI/STAMPI, implemented in STUMPY as `stumpy.stumpi`), and a separate, more scalable streaming-discord algorithm (**DAMP**, 2022) is explicitly designed for ultra-fast online discord detection. However, **no production-quality incremental algorithm exists for the multivariate case** — multivariate MP (mSTUMP/`mstump`) is batch-only in all libraries found; streaming multivariate use would require either running per-channel `stumpy.stumpi` independently (losing cross-channel structure) or periodically recomputing `mstump` over a sliding window.

### Cited Findings
- **STOMPI**: incremental matrix-profile algorithm that updates the MP as new points arrive instead of recomputing from scratch, pre-computing an initial series/MP and then processing incoming points one at a time. Described as "the only existing algorithm capable of computing MP in a true streaming fashion" (as of the LAMP paper), but **not GPU-parallelized**, and its per-update cost **grows slowly over time** as more history accumulates. — [STUMPY stumpi source](https://stumpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/stumpy/stumpi.html), [LAMP paper](https://www.cs.ucr.edu/%7eeamonn/LAMP_Camera_Ready2.pdf)
- **STUMPY's `stumpy.stumpi`** implements online STOMPI/STAMPI for **univariate** series; constructed from an initial series + window size `m`, then updated via `update(t)` per new point; `egress=True` drops the oldest point to keep series length constant (true streaming), `egress=False` lets the series grow. Validated to match batch `stumpy.stump` output. — [STUMPY streaming tutorial](https://stumpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Tutorial_Matrix_Profiles_For_Streaming_Data.html)
- No `mstumpi` (incremental multivariate) function exists in the STUMPY API as documented; the API only lists a batch multi-dimensional function (`mstump`/`mstumped`) and a separate univariate incremental function (`stumpi`). This was confirmed by checking STUMPY's API overview and GitHub discussions; no evidence of a combined streaming+multivariate function was found. — [STUMPY API](https://stumpy.readthedocs.io/en/stable/api.html)
- **DAMP** ("Discord Aware Matrix Profile"), Lu, Wu, Mueen, Zuluaga, Keogh, **"Matrix Profile XXIV: Scaling Time Series Anomaly Detection to Trillions of Datapoints and Ultra-fast Arriving Data Streams,"** KDD 2022 (DOI 10.1145/3534678.3539271); expanded journal version "DAMP: accurate time series anomaly detection on trillions of datapoints and ultra-fast arriving data streams," *Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery* 37 (2023), pp. 627–669 — explicitly targets the two weaknesses of classic discord algorithms: (1) batch-only operation, (2) poor scalability beyond tens of thousands of points. Computes **exact left-discords** (scoring each new point using only past data, suitable for streaming) at **up to 300,000 Hz on a commodity desktop**, enabling discord discovery on datasets with trillions of points. — [KDD paper via Unpaywall](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F3534678.3539271), [long version](https://www.cs.ucr.edu/~eamonn/DAMP_long_version.pdf)
- DAMP is implemented in the **TSB-UAD** benchmark library (adapted from TimeEval), with parameters `m` (window length), `sp_index` (> m), `x_lag` (defaults to `2**ceil(log2(8m))`). Official MATLAB implementation exists; an unofficial Python port is on GitHub (`sihohan/DAMP`). The TSB-UAD docs do **not** confirm multivariate support — the `fit()` signature nominally accepts `(n_samples, n_features)` but the worked example only uses a univariate series, so multivariate use is **unconfirmed**. — [TSB-UAD Discord docs](https://tsb-uad.readthedocs.io/en/latest/algorithms/Discord.html)
- A JPL/NASA paper (Daigavane et al. 2020, see above) explicitly flags that their retrospective (batch) multidimensional-MP evaluation is a necessary precursor to **future** online deployment, and that "the online version of the detectors would likely employ a different peak-finding strategy" — i.e., as of that paper (2020), the online multivariate case was future work, not solved. — [PDF](https://www.wkiri.com/research/papers/daigavane-transitions-MiLeTS-20.pdf)
- A Lisbon thesis specifically proposes applying matrix profile discord detection to "continuous and endless" univariate datastreams — again confirming that the mature streaming treatments in the literature are univariate-first. — [thesis PDF](https://fenix.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/downloadFile/1689244997261241/71003-carlos-branco_resumo.pdf)

### Inferences
- For the project's walk-forward, zero-label, online protocol, the practical path would be: (a) run univariate `stumpy.stumpi` or DAMP independently per channel in a genuinely incremental/online fashion (cheap, well-supported, validated), then fuse scores across the 47 channels with a simple rule (sum/max, as in the Daigavane et al. and dtaianomaly approaches) — **not** true joint multivariate streaming MP, which does not exist in mature form; or (b) periodically recompute batch `mstump` over a rolling window (e.g., monthly), sacrificing true incrementality but capturing cross-channel structure. Given the project's monthly walk-forward folds, option (b) (batch recompute per fold) is arguably a more natural fit to the existing protocol than forcing true point-by-point streaming.
- DAMP is the single most promising "genuinely online" candidate in the literature for cheap per-channel discord scoring at scale, but its lack of confirmed multivariate support means a 47-channel deployment would need either 47 independent DAMP instances (with a fusion step) or a custom multivariate adaptation that was not found to be established elsewhere.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm from the available documentation whether DAMP's left-discord approach, or a per-channel-stumpi approach, has ever been validated against truly **contaminated** training windows (i.e., training data itself containing undetected anomalies, as in this project's zero-label setting) — none of the sources discuss training-data contamination robustness explicitly for either algorithm.
- No numeric per-point latency/throughput figures for `stumpy.stumpi` (only qualitative "cost grows slowly over time" caveat) were found; only DAMP's 300,000 Hz figure is a concrete number, and it is unclear whether that figure is per-channel or whether it would hold at d=47 channels run in parallel.

## Is there a mature Python library (stumpy, matrixprofile-ts, tsmp) suitable for ~47 channels at 18s resolution, monthly folds of ~148,000 rows?

### Takeaway
**STUMPY** is the clear choice: actively maintained, Numba-JIT CPU-parallelized (and GPU/Dask-scalable), with both univariate streaming (`stumpi`) and batch multivariate (`mstump`/`mstumped`) support. The alternatives — **matrixprofile-ts** (Python) and **tsmp** (R) — are comparatively weaker fits: matrixprofile-ts shows no confirmed recent (2024+) maintenance activity, and tsmp is an R package (its Java port `tsmp4j` exists but is a minor project), making it a worse fit for a Python-based benchmark pipeline.

### Cited Findings
- **STUMPY**: "a powerful and scalable library ... used for a variety of time series data mining tasks," Numba-compiled and parallelized via multi-threading and Dask, tested on up to 256 CPU cores across 32 servers or 16 GPUs on one DGX-2 server. — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/stumpy/), [docs](https://stumpy.readthedocs.io/)
- STUMPY computes the matrix profile with the documented complexity of the exact STOMP method, **O(n²)** time (vs. brute force O(n²m) and predecessor STAMP's O(n² log n)), with the matrix profile itself needing only O(n) storage. — [STUMPY tutorial](https://stumpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Tutorial_The_Matrix_Profile.html)
- A competing GPU-focused library, **SCAMP**, disputes STUMPY's published cross-hardware benchmark comparisons and claims pyscamp is 20x+ faster than STUMPY on CPU when run on the same machine — flagged as an interested-party claim, unverified independently, but it signals that published MP library benchmarks should not be taken at face value without re-running them on the target hardware. — [SCAMP docs](https://scamp-docs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/performance.html)
- **matrixprofile-ts**: a Python package under the "Matrix Profile Foundation"; no evidence of 2024+ maintenance activity was found in PyPI release history (search did not surface a deprecation notice either, so status is genuinely unclear, not confirmed-dead). — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/matrixprofile-ts/)
- **tsmp** (R, not Python): actively maintained, latest CRAN release 0.4.16 on 2025-12-04 (removed C++11 requirement), maintained by Francisco Bischoff. A Java port (`tsmp4j`) exists on GitHub listing STOMPI among implemented algorithms, but appears to be a smaller/less-adopted project. — [CRAN tsmp](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/packages/tsmp/readme/README.html), [tsmp4j](https://github.com/eugene-kamenev/tsmp4j)
- **pytsmp**: a third, smaller Python project explicitly described by its own author as "for my own learning... I may not be able to actively develop it from time to time" — not a serious production candidate. — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pytsmp)
- **dtaianomaly** (newer, general-purpose Python anomaly-detection library) ships a ready-made `MatrixProfileDetector` wrapping STOMP with per-dimension-sum multivariate fusion — potentially a faster integration path than hand-rolling fusion logic on top of raw STUMPY. — [dtaianomaly docs](https://dtaianomaly.readthedocs.io/en/0.4.0/_modules/dtaianomaly/anomaly_detection/MatrixProfileDetector.html)

### Inferences
- STUMPY is the only library among those searched with (a) active current maintenance, (b) a Python-native API, (c) both the univariate streaming and multivariate batch pieces needed, and (d) genuine CPU parallelism via Numba — making it the clearly correct implementation choice for this project over matrixprofile-ts or tsmp.
- dtaianomaly is worth evaluating as a thinner wrapper to reduce implementation time, since it already encodes a defensible (if simple) multivariate fusion strategy consistent with what the 2024 Yeh et al. paper found to be competitive.

### Gaps
- No concrete, verifiable wall-clock benchmark table for `stumpy.stump`/`mstump` at series lengths in the 50,000–1,000,000 range on ordinary (non-server, non-GPU) laptop CPU hardware was found via web search; STUMPY's own documentation pages did not yield extractable timing tables in this session (see computational cost section below for how this gap was partially bridged via an independent empirical data point).

## Is Matrix Profile actually used in real industrial/production anomaly detection, and are there sensor/IoT/spacecraft case studies?

### Takeaway
Matrix profile has real traction in **research-grade industrial and spacecraft case studies** (JPL/NASA, CNC manufacturing, chemical-plant simulation, acoustic/vibration monitoring context), and a NASA JPL study found MP to be the **best-performing method** for a genuine spacecraft (Cassini) anomaly/event-detection task — but no source found describes a long-running, continuously-operating **production** deployment (as opposed to retrospective/offline research studies) with reported operational outcomes, on spacecraft telemetry or otherwise.

### Cited Findings
- **Strongest spacecraft evidence**: Daigavane, Wagstaff, Doran, Cochrane, Jackman, Rymer, "Detection of Environment Transitions in Time Series Data for Responsive Science," MileTS '20 (KDD workshop), 2020. JPL researchers applied a per-channel-sum multivariate matrix profile (and HOT-SAX, RuLSIF, Bayesian/non-Bayesian HMM) to 31-dimensional Cassini CAPS-ELS plasma-spectrometer data (2-second cadence, 2004–2012, 2,339 labeled magnetic-boundary-crossing events) to detect magnetospheric environment transitions, as a testbed for the planned PIMS instrument on the Europa Clipper mission. **At a required precision of 0.8, Matrix Profile was the best-performing method for bow-shock crossing detection, achieving recall 0.636** (vs. non-Bayesian HMM's 0.583); for magnetopause crossings the non-Bayesian HMM won overall and no method reached 0.8 precision reliably across years. The authors explicitly frame this as a candidate for future **onboard** (in-situ, computationally constrained) deployment, not yet realized operationally — next steps listed include porting to C and testing on a PPC750 (the Europa Clipper's planned flight processor class) and radiation-sensitivity testing. — [PDF](https://www.wkiri.com/research/papers/daigavane-transitions-MiLeTS-20.pdf)
- Same paper reports a concrete runtime data point: Matrix Profile took an average of about **8 minutes to process a single 6-hour, 31-dimensional observation** (≈10,800 time steps/dimension at 2s cadence, window w=100) on a 2.1-GHz processor — substantially cheaper than RuLSIF (40 min to >5 hours per 6-hour window) but much slower than HOT-SAX or either HMM variant (<1 minute each). — [PDF](https://www.wkiri.com/research/papers/daigavane-transitions-MiLeTS-20.pdf)
- A related NASA/JPL connection noted but not independently verified in this session: the UCR Matrix Profile page references a Wagstaff et al. study applying MP to InSight Mars seismic data ("Marsquakes"), suggesting continued JPL interest in MP for planetary/space mission telemetry beyond the Cassini study. — [UCR Matrix Profile page](https://cs.ucr.edu/~eamonn/MatrixProfile.html)
- **Industrial/manufacturing case studies** (research-grade, not confirmed production): a Korean-language study applies matrix profile to multivariate CNC-machine sensor data (spindle load, spindle speed, servo motor load) sampled at 0.1s to extract recurring patterns and flag anomalies; a 2020 Polish conference paper tests matrix profiles on unlabeled industrial IoT sensor data specifically to assess "practicality for production maintenance," concluding results are promising but exploratory; a 2022 Springer book chapter proposes combining expert domain knowledge with matrix-profile anomaly detection for predictive maintenance, describing early results as "encouraging" (i.e., not yet a mature deployment). A 2019 AIChE conference abstract applies matrix profile to a **simulated** (not live) vinyl-acetate-monomer chemical plant dataset. — [Korean CNC study](https://koreascience.kr/article/CFKO202232558440344.pdf), [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-17030-0_3), [AIChE abstract](https://aiche.org/conferences/aiche-spring-meeting-and-global-congress-on-process-safety/2019/proceeding/paper/191c-application-matrix-profile-efficient-large-scale-data-mining-operational-time-series-data)
- Keogh group's own framing in **DAMP** (KDD 2022) explicitly states that classic time-series discord detection, "a relatively simple twenty-year old distance-based technique, remains among the state-of-art techniques" for anomaly detection generally — an indicator of continued relevance/credibility in the research community, though this is a claim from the method's own authors, not an independent industrial source. — [Unpaywall link](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F3534678.3539271)
- A Springer paper on "practical joint human-machine exploration of industrial time series" uses matrix profile on **three real-life industrial datasets** to let users extract semantic information, detect cycles, and find deviating patterns — framed as an interactive/human-in-the-loop tool rather than an autonomous production anomaly detector. — [Springer](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/practical-joint-human-machine-exploration-of-industrial-time-ser/23575288)

### Inferences
- The evidence supports "matrix profile is a credible, research-validated candidate that has been specifically tested on spacecraft telemetry and found competitive/best-in-class in at least one real mission dataset (Cassini)" — this is a stronger case than most other "classical" baselines would have. However, it falls short of "used in real industrial/production anomaly detection deployments" in the sense of a continuously-running operational system with reported production outcomes; all sources found are research studies, pilots, or conceptual proposals, several explicitly describing results as "encouraging" or "promising" rather than deployed.
- The lack of confirmed production deployment should not be read as a mark against feasibility — it's arguably typical for most of the "classical" time-series anomaly detection literature, including methods the project may consider as comparators.

### Gaps
- No vendor/product-level, operationally-reported case study with hard production metrics (uptime, false-positive rate in live operation, etc.) was found; several results that looked promising (Industrial Matrix blog posts on coffee-roasting and unnamed plant case studies) were explicitly identified as marketing content without disclosed methodology and were excluded as unreliable.
- Did not find any case study combining matrix profile with explicitly **contaminated, unlabeled** training data (the core constraint of this project) — all industrial/spacecraft case studies found either use labeled evaluation data or assume clean baseline/reference periods.

## What is the realistic computational cost at ~47 channels, ~148,000 rows/month, 18s resolution, on a laptop CPU?

### Takeaway
The exact matrix profile is **O(n²) per channel** (equivalently **O(n²d)** for d channels processed independently-then-fused, which is the recommended multivariate strategy from question 1). At this project's scale (n≈148,000/month, d=47), this is **computationally heavy but very plausibly tractable per fold** if implemented with STUMPY's Numba/JIT-parallelized STOMP rather than a naive Python implementation — likely tens of minutes to a few hours per monthly fold on a multi-core laptop CPU — but **not free**, and will dominate runtime compared to the project's existing lightweight baselines (ECOD/COPOD/IForest, Kalman filter) if run across many monthly folds, multiple window-length hyperparameters, and/or multiple missions.

### Cited Findings
- STOMP's complexity is consistently reported as **O(n²)** time, O(n) space for the matrix profile itself (vs. brute force O(n²m), and predecessor STAMP's O(n² log n)). — [STUMPY tutorial](https://stumpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Tutorial_The_Matrix_Profile.html)
- The 2024 Yeh et al. multivariate paper gives explicit complexities for channel-fusion strategies: **post-sorting/post-max: O(n₁n₂d)**; **pre-sorting: O(n₁n₂d log d)**; space O(max(n₁,n₂)·d); their proposed k-NN extension is O(n₁n₂d) (vs. brute force O(k·n₁n₂d)). For a self-join, n₁=n₂=n, so this reduces to O(n²d), matching the Daigavane et al. figure. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09298)
- Daigavane et al. (JPL, 2020) state the same multivariate complexity explicitly: **"Computing the Matrix Profiles for each dimension takes O(n²) time per dimension, for a total of O(n²d) time."** — [PDF](https://www.wkiri.com/research/papers/daigavane-transitions-MiLeTS-20.pdf)
- **Only concrete, independently-reproducible empirical data point found**: the same JPL paper reports ~8 minutes of wall-clock time to compute the (31-dimensional, per-channel-sum) matrix profile over a 6-hour, 2-second-cadence observation (≈10,800 points/channel, window w=100) on a 2.1 GHz CPU — implying roughly n²d ≈ 10,800² × 31 ≈ 3.65×10⁹ "distance-profile units" computed in ~480 seconds (≈7.6×10⁶ units/sec), though it is **not stated whether this implementation used the fast STOMP incremental-dot-product trick or a slower approach**, so this throughput figure should be treated as a lower bound on what an optimized implementation (e.g., STUMPY) could achieve, not a ceiling. — [PDF](https://www.wkiri.com/research/papers/daigavane-transitions-MiLeTS-20.pdf)
- No concrete STUMPY wall-clock benchmark table for CPU at series lengths in the 50,000–1,000,000 range was retrievable in this session (search and fetch attempts on STUMPY's own documentation pages did not surface the performance table's actual numbers — the page exists per prior citations in this research but its content did not parse). The one fully legible number found was the GPU-STOMP baseline reproduced from the original Matrix Profile II paper (Zhu et al., ICDM 2016): **n=100,000,000 points, window m=50, took ~291 hours on an NVIDIA Tesla K80 GPU** — not directly informative for this project's much smaller (n≈148,000) and CPU-only setting, but confirms that the O(n²) term truly dominates runtime at scale (291 hrs for 100M points vs. minutes for datasets several orders of magnitude smaller). — [search result citing STUMPY docs]
- A competing library (SCAMP) claims 20x+ CPU speedups over STUMPY, an unverified, interested-party claim, but suggests meaningful headroom exists beyond STUMPY's out-of-the-box numbers if runtime becomes a bottleneck. — [SCAMP docs](https://scamp-docs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/performance.html)

### Inferences (explicitly flagged as back-of-envelope extrapolation, not a cited benchmark)
- Scaling the JPL 8-minute/6-hour/31-channel/2.1GHz data point by n²d to this project's scale (n≈148,000 vs 10,800 → ratio ≈188x in n, so ≈35,100x in n²; d=47 vs 31 → ratio ≈1.5x) gives a **naive estimate of roughly 35,100 × 1.5 × 8 minutes ≈ 702,000 minutes (~487 days) on a single core at the same (likely unoptimized) throughput** — clearly infeasible if implemented the way the JPL paper did it.
- However, STUMPY's STOMP implementation uses the standard incremental-dot-product trick (O(1) amortized update per cell) plus Numba JIT compilation and multi-core parallelism, which is known from the GPU-STOMP paper's own framing to be many orders of magnitude faster than naive nested-loop computation of the same O(n²) asymptotic complexity — the JPL paper's 8-minute figure is almost certainly **not** using this optimized approach (no mention of STUMPY, Numba, or vectorization in the method description). A realistic expectation, consistent with STUMPY's documented support for exact MP computation on "significantly more than a few thousand data points" on ordinary hardware with optional GPU/Dask scale-out for larger problems, is that a single monthly fold (n≈148,000, run per-channel across 47 channels, i.e. 47 independent 1-D STOMP computations each O(n²) ≈ 2.19×10¹⁰) is **tractable on a multi-core laptop CPU in a timeframe of minutes to a few hours**, not days — but this is an extrapolation and should be validated empirically by timing `stumpy.stump` on the project's actual data before committing to it as a baseline across many walk-forward folds.
- Running this across many monthly folds (the project's experiment files suggest ~15-20+ walk-forward folds already) and potentially multiple window-length (m) hyperparameters compounds the cost linearly; this is the single biggest practical risk — not any one fold being infeasible, but the **total compute budget across the full walk-forward × hyperparameter-sweep protocol** potentially running into many hours to low-single-digit days of CPU time, which is a materially higher cost than the project's already-implemented lightweight baselines (ECOD/COPOD/IForest/Kalman).

### Gaps
- No directly reproducible, hardware-specified STUMPY benchmark at n≈100,000–200,000 was found; the computational feasibility conclusion above rests on an extrapolation from one external data point (JPL paper, likely unoptimized) and general library documentation claims, not a direct benchmark at this project's scale. **Strong recommendation for the report: before adopting matrix profile as a new baseline, run a short empirical timing test of `stumpy.stump` (univariate, per-channel) and `stumpy.mstump` on one real month of the project's actual 47-channel, 18s-resolution data on the target laptop CPU, rather than relying on this literature-based extrapolation.**
- Could not determine whether the `x_lag`/window-size choices typical in this literature (e.g., w=50–400 in the JPL study) would need to be substantially larger for 18-second-resolution satellite telemetry (where meaningful anomaly durations may span many more raw samples than in the 2-second-cadence Cassini study), which would directly inflate runtime further since window size does not appear in the O(n²) formula explicitly but affects the constant factor and the meaningfulness of results.
