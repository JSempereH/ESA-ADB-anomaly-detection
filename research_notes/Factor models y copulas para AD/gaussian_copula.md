# Gaussian-Copula (and other explicit joint-copula) Methods for Multivariate, Contaminated, Online Anomaly Detection on Real Sensor/Satellite Telemetry

## Q1: Is there published work doing a Gaussian copula (or other parametric joint copula) built ON TOP OF empirical marginal CDFs, specifically for anomaly/outlier detection on multivariate data — ideally industrial/sensor/time-series, not just tabular/financial?

### Takeaway
No published method does exactly "ECOD/COPOD-style empirical marginal CDFs → inverse-normal transform → fitted Gaussian correlation matrix → Mahalanobis/log-likelihood score." The closest real analogs either (a) stay with a fully non-parametric empirical copula and skip the Gaussian step (COPOD itself), or (b) go further than Gaussian into full vine-copula models with *parametric* (not empirical) marginals fitted by offline MLE (Horváth et al. 2020). Nothing in the literature search sits exactly at the specific midpoint this project is proposing.

### Cited Findings
- COPOD ("Copula-Based Outlier Detection," Li, Zhao, Botta, Ionescu, Hu; arXiv:2009.09463, ICDM 2020, DOI 10.1109/ICDM50108.2020.00135) builds an **empirical copula** from per-dimension empirical CDFs and predicts tail probabilities ("anomalous p-values") per point; it is parameter-free and does not fit a parametric (Gaussian/t) dependence structure at all — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.09463v1), [PIK Potsdam record](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_24536).
- ECOD ("Unsupervised Outlier Detection Using Empirical Cumulative Distribution Functions," Li, Zhao, Hu, Botta, Ionescu, Chen; arXiv:2201.00382, IEEE TKDE 35(12):12181-12193, DOI 10.1109/TKDE.2022.3159580) computes per-dimension empirical-CDF tail probabilities and **explicitly states as a stated limitation that it ignores how features relate to one another**, and its own authors suggest **future work using vine copulas or probabilistic graphical models to model feature dependencies** — [paperswithcode](https://astro.paperswithcode.com/paper/ecod-unsupervised-outlier-detection-using), [PIK Potsdam record](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_26927).
- Inspection of the actual PyOD ECOD source (`pyod/models/ecod.py`) confirms the mechanism matches the project's own description: per-column empirical CDF → `U_l = -log(ECDF(X))`, `U_r = -log(ECDF(-X))`, a skewness-selected per-dimension term, and a **plain `.sum(axis=1)` across dimensions with no covariance or joint-probability term anywhere in the code** — [pyod docs](https://pyod.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/pyod/models/ecod.html).
- "Copula-based anomaly scoring and localization for large-scale, high-dimensional continuous data" (Horváth, Kovács, Molontay, Nováczki; arXiv:1912.02166, ACM Transactions on Intelligent Systems and Technology 11(3), 2020) is the closest published work to a genuine joint-copula anomaly detector on real sensor-like (telecom network performance-counter) data, but it is a **full vine copula** (Chow-Liu tree + per-edge pair-copulas selected by likelihood among Archimedean/elliptical/non-parametric families, fit with `vinecopulib`), with **parametric marginals** (exponential, Student-t, log-normal, Pareto, Gaussian/hyper-Erlang/beta mixtures, chosen by per-variable likelihood, hyper-Erlang winning most often) — not empirical marginals, and not a Gaussian-only copula — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.02166), [real.mtak.hu record](https://real.mtak.hu/186581).
- "CoCAI: Copula-based Conformal Anomaly Identification for Multivariate Time-Series" (Pearson, Zanello, Russo, Bortolussi, Cairoli; arXiv:2507.17796, accepted at Runtime Verification 2025, Springer DOI 10.1007/978-3-032-05435-7_17) uses a **Student's t-copula** (not Gaussian) wrapped in a conformal-prediction framework, validated on **real operational data from water distribution and sewerage systems** — a genuine real industrial sensor-stream validation, though not satellite telemetry — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17796).
- A US patent ("Unified analytics engine," downloadable PDF) describes an analytics system that explicitly combines a COPOD-style empirical-copula score (left-tail, right-tail, and skew-corrected negative-log terms) applied to a "satellite pass signal," but this is a patent filing, not a peer-reviewed validation, and does not use a Gaussian correlation step — [USPTO PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12184394).
- A separate US patent on cyber-threat detection explicitly models exceedance probabilities with a **Gaussian copula**, thresholds to a restricted exceedance set, transforms to normal scores, estimates a correlation matrix, and suggests a **lasso penalty on off-diagonal correlation entries for sparsity** — structurally the closest patent-level analog to the project's proposed pipeline, but again not peer-reviewed and not validated on sensor/telemetry data — [USPTO PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11470103).

### Inferences
- The "ECOD/COPOD empirical-CDF marginals + Gaussian-copula Mahalanobis/log-likelihood joint score" pipeline sits in a genuine gap between two published extremes: COPOD/ECOD (fully non-parametric, fully independent) and Horváth et al. 2020 / vine-copula process-monitoring work (genuinely joint, but parametric marginals + full vine structure, fit offline). No source found fills that specific middle ground.
- ECOD's own authors naming "vine copulas or probabilistic graphical models" as the natural next step for exactly the limitation this project identified (independence assumption) is a meaningful piece of validation that the research direction is sound and recognized by the original authors — not merely a project-local observation.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm via full-text search whether any paper explicitly combines *empirical* (ECDF-based, parameter-free) marginals with a *Gaussian* (not vine, not t) copula for anomaly detection on any data type, industrial or otherwise. Absence of evidence here is notable given how natural the combination seems, and increases confidence it would be a novel combination (see Q2).

## Q2: Is the "ECOD/COPOD empirical marginal CDF → inverse-normal-transform → fit joint Gaussian correlation matrix → Mahalanobis/log-likelihood score" pipeline a known, named method?

### Takeaway
This exact pipeline does not appear to be a published, named method. It would be a **novel combination** this project would be assembling itself from well-established individual pieces (empirical-CDF marginals from ECOD/COPOD; the normal-quantile/probability-integral transform and Gaussian-copula correlation fit from the classical copula literature and from Zhao & Udell's online Gaussian-copula work).

### Cited Findings
- No search surfaced a named method matching this pipeline. The closest structural match found was the cyber-threat-detection patent (see Q1), which is unpublished/non-peer-reviewed and not validated on sensor data — [USPTO PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11470103).
- The general mathematical building blocks are well established independently: the probability-integral-transform / rank-based normal-score construction `z_i = Φ⁻¹(u_i)`, `u_i = r_i/(n+1)` is documented generically (not as an anomaly-detection method) at [MetricGate's Gaussian Copula Model reference](https://metricgate.com/calculator/gaussian-copula-model).
- Zhao, Landgrebe, Shekhtman, Udell, "Online Missing Value Imputation and Change Point Detection with the Gaussian Copula" (AAAI-22, Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence 36(8):9199-9207, DOI 10.1609/aaai.v36i8.20906; preprint arXiv:2009.12326) fits a Gaussian copula **incrementally/online** to streaming mixed-type data (continuous, ordinal, boolean) and uses the fitted copula for **change-point detection** in the dependence structure — this is the closest published "Gaussian copula fit online" machinery, but it targets imputation and change-point detection, not per-row anomaly scoring, and it assumes MCAR missingness rather than contamination-by-anomalies — [AAAI OJS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/20906), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.12326v2).

### Inferences
- Because the component parts (empirical marginal CDF, probability-integral transform to normal scores, Gaussian-copula correlation fit, online/incremental covariance update) are each independently well-published, but no source assembles them into a single named anomaly-score pipeline, the project should describe this explicitly as **a novel combination it is assembling from established primitives**, not cite it as replicating a specific paper's method. The correct framing for a related-work section is: "we extend the ECOD/COPOD empirical-marginal framework with an explicit Gaussian-copula dependence step, a combination motivated by [cite ECOD's own stated limitation] and structurally analogous to the online Gaussian-copula machinery of Zhao & Udell (2022), but to our knowledge not previously applied to per-observation anomaly scoring."

### Gaps
- Did not exhaustively search non-English-language literature (e.g., Chinese process-monitoring journals, several of which appeared in Q1/Q4 vine-copula searches) which may contain a closer match; flagged as a residual risk rather than a confirmed gap.

## Q3: Is there a genuinely robust way to estimate the joint Gaussian copula's correlation matrix from CONTAMINATED (unfiltered, L2) data online/incrementally? Is rank-based (Spearman) correlation inherently more robust to this kind of contamination than raw Pearson sample covariance, or does it have its own distinct failure modes?

### Takeaway
Plain Spearman rank correlation is **not** a high-breakdown-point estimator and should not be assumed automatically robust to contamination despite being rank-based; it is a meaningful improvement over raw Pearson on heavy-tailed/scaled contamination (because ranks cap the influence of magnitude-extreme outliers) but can still be distorted by outliers that are extreme in *rank position* rather than magnitude, and by cellwise (as opposed to casewise) contamination. Genuinely robust alternatives exist (Gaussian rank correlation / van der Waerden scores, Tyler's M-estimator with shrinkage, wrapping transformations) but these are more complex than plain Spearman and were mostly validated only in batch/offline settings with moderate (≤50%) contamination fractions.

### Cited Findings
- A 2006-era robustness study and a 2023 *Mathematics and Statistics* paper on hybrid Spearman/MM-estimators both state that **Pearson's, Spearman's, and Kendall's coefficients are all "sufficiently non-robust against outliers," i.e., none has a high breakdown point** — [search synthesis citing both](https://acikerisim.gelisim.edu.tr/items/721484e5-9666-4c43-b282-fa8165b295fe/full).
- A paper on robust high-dimensional precision-matrix estimation (arXiv, exact ID not independently re-confirmed in this pass) describes **Gaussian rank correlation** (built from van der Waerden normal scores rather than raw ranks) as robust and asymptotically as efficient as Pearson at the normal model, and notes that both the Spearman and Gaussian-rank correlation matrices remain positive semi-definite by construction (computed as a covariance of ranks/scores), avoiding the need for PSD-repair steps (NPD, OGK) that plague some robust covariance estimators — [arXiv:1501.01219](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1501.01219).
- The same source reports that using a robust scale estimator (Q_n) on top of rank correlation can achieve a **50% breakdown point under cellwise contamination** — [arXiv:1501.01219](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1501.01219).
- An MM-estimator-based hybrid Spearman ("MM-Spearman") was Monte-Carlo tested under 10%-50% contamination and reported lower MSE/RMSE than classical Spearman, but the authors explicitly note the method **cannot overcome contamination above 50%** by construction — [Gelisim University record](https://avesis.gelisim.edu.tr/yayin/e63b19ed-4a5f-4c3b-9db0-bf50665d9ecd/hybrid-correlation-coefficient-of-spearman-with-mm-estimator).
- A thesis-level review of high-dimensional robust correlation estimators (Pearson, Spearman, MCD, SSCM, GK, and the wrapping transform of Raymaekers & Rousseeuw 2021) finds wrapping preserves PSD correlation matrices with good robustness properties, but its own simulations only tested contamination up to 10% — gap noted by the reviewing source itself.
- Chen, Wiesel, and Hero (IEEE Trans. Signal Processing, 2011) build a **shrinkage version of Tyler's M-estimator** for high-dimensional, small-sample covariance estimation with a closed-form, data-dependent shrinkage coefficient (MMSE-derived), and validate it on **activity/intrusion detection in a wireless sensor network** — a real sensor-network application, though the published abstract describes a batch fit, not an incremental/streaming update.
- A 2026 arXiv preprint on "Online robust covariance matrix estimation and outlier detection" explicitly positions itself as filling the gap that classical shrinkage methods (Ledoit-Wolf 2004, Cabana et al. 2021) are **not online**, while its own method is robust-Mahalanobis-based and online — the single closest match found to "robust + online + outlier-aware covariance," though it is a preprint and framed around covariance (Pearson-space) rather than copula rank-correlation — [arXiv:2601.03957](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.03957) (note: this arXiv ID's year prefix implies a very recent/2026 preprint; treat as unreviewed).

### Inferences
- For this project's specific prior failure mode (a few extreme rows inflating raw Pearson sample covariance ~100,000x), switching to **rank-based** correlation (Spearman, or better, Gaussian rank/van der Waerden normal scores) should substantially mitigate that specific magnitude-driven blowup, because the rank transform caps each observation's influence to its rank regardless of how extreme its raw value is — this is a structural, not merely empirical, advantage directly relevant to the project's documented bug.
- However, Spearman/rank correlation is not a cure-all: a contamination fraction large enough to shift *rank order* itself (not just magnitude) can still bias it, and it has no formal high breakdown point. For a first implementation, plain Spearman (or the already-available empirical-CDF ranks ECOD/COPOD compute) is a reasonable, cheap, closed-form choice; a documented stretch goal would be Gaussian rank correlation (trivial extra step: apply inverse-normal transform to the ranks before computing covariance) for closer alignment with the normal-model efficiency guarantee.

### Gaps
- No source found directly tests rank-based/Spearman correlation robustness specifically in an **online/incremental, walk-forward** satellite-telemetry-like setting with contamination rates comparable to this project's (unknown exact anomaly rate in training windows, but ESA-ADB Mission 2 anomalies are a small minority of rows). The 10-50% contamination regimes in the cited studies may not match this project's (likely much lower, e.g., single-digit percent) contamination rate, which if true would make even plain Spearman comfortably robust enough — this project would need to verify its own actual per-window contamination rate to know which regime applies.

## Q4: Beyond Gaussian copulas, are vine copulas or other non-Gaussian joint copula families (e.g. t-copula) used for anomaly detection on real multivariate industrial/sensor data — and is there a CPU-feasible online-capable implementation path, or does estimation require offline optimization that would break this project's closed-form preference?

### Takeaway
Vine copulas and t-copulas are actively used for industrial fault/anomaly detection (mostly chemical-process monitoring, e.g. Tennessee Eastman benchmark, plus some real multivariate sensor streams), but essentially all published implementations fit the model **offline** via combinatorial structure search (vine tree selection) plus per-edge maximum-likelihood/numerical optimization — none of the sources found describe a genuinely incremental/streaming vine or t-copula fit. This would break the project's closed-form/CPU-feasible-in-a-few-hours constraint if pursued now.

### Cited Findings
- "Localized Anomaly Detection via Differentiable D-vine Copulas" (arXiv:2607.25020, preprint, 2026) uses **gradient-based maximum likelihood with beam search** to fit the D-vine structure, explicitly motivated by the fact that "existing greedy procedures can miss better global fits" because configurations grow combinatorially — confirming that even the newest work treats vine-structure fitting as a non-trivial offline optimization problem. It adds Mondrian conformal prediction for statistical guarantees and produces edge-level (per-variable-pair) anomaly explanations — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25020), [project page](https://www.roboticscenter.ai/research/papers/localized-anomaly-detection-via-differentiable-d-vine-copulas-2607).
- "Nonlinear and Non-Gaussian Process Monitoring Based on Simplified R-Vine Copula" (2018) explicitly notes that **"the number of pair copulas to be optimized and the parameters to be estimated increase rapidly when the dimensionality of the variables is large,"** motivating a simplification step (via a Vuong test) purely to make fitting tractable — direct confirmation that full vine-copula fitting does not scale cheaply with channel count (this project has 47 channels) — [AGRIS/FAO record](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65dedf98b766d82b18fea7cf).
- A 2021 *Process Safety and Environmental Protection* paper uses R-vine copulas with an event tree for dynamic risk profiling, benchmarked on both the synthetic Tennessee Eastman process and a **real industrial isomer separator unit**, reporting better performance than PCA and transfer-entropy baselines — a genuine real (non-satellite) industrial multivariate validation of vine copulas for process monitoring.
- A 2019 AIChE conference paper combines a vine-copula model with a robust auto-encoder for fault monitoring, validated on the Tennessee Eastman benchmark (synthetic, not real plant data) — [AIChE record](https://www.aiche.org/conferences/aiche-annual-meeting/2019/proceeding/paper/371e-nonlinear-and-non-gaussian-process-monitoring-based-on-vine-copula-and-robust-auto-encoder).
- Horváth et al. 2020 (see Q1) confirms vine-copula fitting via `vinecopulib` is done in **batch on i.i.d. observations**, with the paper making no claim of online/incremental capability and no treatment of contaminated-training-set robustness — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.02166).
- CoCAI (arXiv:2507.17796) uses a **Student's t-copula** specifically because, unlike Gaussian, it captures symmetric tail dependence via its degrees-of-freedom parameter (lower df = stronger joint-extreme dependence, converging to Gaussian as df→∞), and notes the squared Mahalanobis-type distance under a multivariate-t model follows a scaled F-distribution rather than chi-squared — directly relevant if the project's sensor spikes show joint-extreme (tail-dependent) behavior that a Gaussian copula would miss — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.17796).
- Domino (2020, *Physica A*) models cross-correlated extreme returns with a t-copula plus 4th-order multivariate cumulants for outlier detection, but only tested on **artificial/synthetic financial data**, not real sensor data — [IITiS record](https://iitis.pl/en/node/2676), [arXiv:1804.00541](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.00541).

### Inferences
- A genuine vine-copula extension is not a "few hours" project: structure selection (even simplified/truncated), per-edge family selection among 8+ Archimedean/elliptical candidates, and MLE fitting is inherently a batch/offline combinatorial-plus-numerical-optimization problem at 47 channels, confirmed by multiple independent sources explicitly flagging dimensionality as the scaling bottleneck.
- A **t-copula** (single global family, no per-edge structure search) is a much smaller step up from Gaussian than a vine: it only adds one scalar degrees-of-freedom parameter to the same correlation-matrix machinery, and could plausibly still be closed-form-ish (correlation matrix as before, plus a 1-D numerical search or simple heuristic for df). This is a more realistic "next-next" step than a full vine, if the Gaussian copula shows residual tail-dependence problems in practice.

### Gaps
- No source found gives a concrete wall-clock or big-O comparison of vine/t-copula fitting cost specifically at ~47 dimensions and the project's walk-forward window sizes, so the "a few hours to implement" judgment for vine copulas specifically is an inference from the structural dimensionality warnings cited above, not a directly measured benchmark.

## Q5: Has any copula-based (Gaussian or otherwise) multivariate anomaly detector been validated on real spacecraft/satellite telemetry specifically, or on comparable real industrial multivariate sensor streams (not synthetic, not financial)? Any 2023-2026 papers?

### Takeaway
No copula-based anomaly detector was found validated specifically on real spacecraft/satellite telemetry. The closest real validations of genuine joint-copula anomaly detection are on other real industrial multivariate sensor/telemetry-like streams: a real mobile-telecom network's performance counters (Horváth et al. 2020) and real water-distribution/sewerage operational data (CoCAI, 2025). Separately, copula-*entropy* (not full copula anomaly scoring) has been applied directly to real satellite telemetry for causal/fault-propagation analysis, and a COPOD-style empirical-copula score appears in a patent applied to a "satellite pass signal," but neither is a peer-reviewed joint-copula anomaly detector validated on spacecraft data.

### Cited Findings
- Horváth, Kovács, Molontay, Nováczki (ACM TIST 2020, arXiv:1912.02166) validate their vine-copula anomaly/localization method on **real performance-counter data from a mobile telecommunications network** (not satellite, but a comparable real large-scale multivariate industrial sensor stream) — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.02166).
- CoCAI (Pearson et al., 2025, arXiv:2507.17796) validates its t-copula conformal anomaly method on **real operational data from water distribution and sewerage systems** — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17796).
- A 2025 arXiv review on "Copula Entropy: Theory and Applications" (arXiv:2512.18168) reports that Liu et al. applied **copula-entropy-based transfer entropy directly to real satellite telemetry**, producing fault-propagation diagrams between telemetry parameters that outperformed traditional transfer-entropy methods, and that Zeng et al. introduced an improved copula-entropy-based causal-network metric (NMCTE) for satellite telemetry, feeding a "CN-FA-LSTM" anomaly detector — this is copula-entropy for causal structure discovery, not a copula-based joint anomaly *score*, but it is a direct real-satellite-telemetry application of copula mathematics — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.18168).
- A US patent ("Unified analytics engine") describes a COPOD-style empirical-copula outlier score (left-tail, right-tail, skew-corrected negative-log terms) explicitly applied to a "satellite pass signal" — but this is an unreviewed patent filing, not a validated research result, and uses the independence-assuming empirical-copula formulation (same as COPOD), not a genuine joint dependency model — [USPTO PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12184394).
- Separately (non-copula, but relevant context for what real spacecraft-telemetry joint-dependency work exists): Pang et al., "Temporal dependence Mahalanobis distance for anomaly detection in multivariate spacecraft telemetry series" (ISA Transactions, vol. 140, listed 2023) explicitly targets joint cross-channel dependency via a Mahalanobis-type distance on real multivariate spacecraft telemetry — structurally analogous to this project's own Kalman-Mahalanobis extension, and a useful related-work citation for "joint dependency helps on real spacecraft telemetry" even though it is not copula-based.
- Katsube & Sahara (IJPHM, published June 2024) build a telemetry monitoring system using Mahalanobis-distance-based features to explain/localize which telemetry channel caused an anomaly, tested on artificial and practical/real abnormal telemetry datasets — again Mahalanobis/covariance-based, not copula-based, but real/practical telemetry data — [PHM Society](https://papers.phmsociety.org/index.php/ijphm/article/view/3945).
- The project's own benchmark context: OPS-SAT (arXiv:2407.04730) and ESA-ADB (arXiv:2406.17826, and its 2025 deployability follow-up arXiv:2607.07335) are confirmed as the main real-satellite-telemetry benchmarks in the current literature; neither benchmark paper's baseline suite was confirmed (in this search pass) to include a copula-based detector.

### Inferences
- The complete absence of a published copula-based detector validated on real spacecraft telemetry means this project would be a genuine first/early contribution on that specific intersection (copula + real satellite data) if it proceeds — strengthening the case for including it in future-work framing even if the Gaussian-copula variant itself isn't implemented immediately.
- The real validations that do exist (telecom counters, water/sewerage) support the claim that joint-copula methods generalize beyond finance to real industrial multivariate streams, which is the correct citation basis for the paper's related-work section rather than claiming satellite-specific precedent.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether the OPS-SAT or ESA-ADB benchmark papers' own baseline suites include COPOD/ECOD or any copula variant (this project already uses ECOD/COPOD operationally, so it likely already knows this, but it was not independently re-verified in this research pass).
- Could not obtain full-text confirmation of the exact Pang et al. ISA Transactions method (only title/venue found, not method detail), so the "Mahalanobis on real spacecraft telemetry" claim is based on title/abstract-level evidence only.

## Q6: Realistic CPU implementation effort estimate (hours) for a Gaussian-copula extension built directly on top of the project's existing ECOD-style empirical-CDF machinery

### Takeaway
This is an engineering estimate, not a literature-sourced fact (noted explicitly per research guidelines — no source estimates implementation hours for this specific combination). Given that the project already has working, validated empirical-CDF marginal computation (via its ECOD/COPOD usage) and has already solved the harder, analogous problem once (robust covariance estimation for the Kalman-Mahalanobis extension, including the specific ~100,000x contamination-inflation bug), a Gaussian-copula extension built on the same marginals is plausibly a **1-2 day (roughly 6-12 hour)** effort, not a multi-week one — provided the project reuses its existing robust-covariance fix rather than re-deriving it, and does not attempt the vine/t-copula extensions (which Q4 shows are a much larger, offline-optimization-heavy effort).

### Cited Findings
- (No external source gives an hours estimate for this specific task; this section is inference-only per the research protocol, reported as an inference rather than a cited finding.)

### Inferences
- Breaking the new work into its components, informed directly by the literature above:
  1. **Reuse existing marginal CDFs** (already computed by ECOD/COPOD in this project) — ~0 hours, assuming the per-dimension empirical CDF values (or equivalent rank/tail-probability arrays) are already extractable from the existing ECOD implementation rather than needing to be recomputed.
  2. **Inverse-normal transform** `z = Φ⁻¹(clip(u, ε, 1-ε))` — trivial, well-defined, ~1 hour including edge-case clipping near 0/1 (needed because `Φ⁻¹(0)` and `Φ⁻¹(1)` are undefined; COPOD/ECOD's own tail-probability formulation already works in log-space, which helps avoid this).
  3. **Fit a robust correlation matrix on contaminated L2 data** — this is the part with real risk, directly informed by Q3: plain Pearson on the normal-scores is exactly the failure mode that already bit the Kalman extension (~100,000x inflation from a few extreme rows), so the project should go straight to rank-based (Spearman, or Gaussian rank/van der Waerden) correlation on the *pre-transform* uniform marginals, or apply the same robust-covariance fix already built for Kalman to the post-transform normal scores. Given the fix already exists in the codebase for the analogous Kalman problem, adapting it is estimated at ~2-4 hours (mostly plumbing and validation, not new math).
  4. **Mahalanobis / multivariate-Gaussian log-likelihood scoring** on the fitted correlation matrix — standard closed-form linear algebra (Cholesky solve or precision-matrix matmul), ~1-2 hours including numerical stability (regularizing/shrinking the correlation matrix to guarantee positive-definiteness, same shrinkage-toward-identity idea used for Ledoit-Wolf-style estimators, Q3).
  5. **Walk-forward integration, per-mission/per-subsystem block-vs-global correlation choice (mirroring the existing Kalman block/global design), and validation against the benchmark harness** — ~2-4 hours, by direct analogy to how long the already-implemented Kalman block/global Mahalanobis extension took in this codebase (not independently verified here, but the existing `kalman_block_multivariate_l2.json` / `kalman_local_trend_l2.json` experiment artifacts referenced in the project's git status indicate this pattern is already a working template to copy).
- Total: roughly **6-12 hours** of focused work, concentrated almost entirely in step 3 (robust correlation estimation) and step 5 (harness integration), both of which have a working precedent already in this codebase (Kalman extension) that can be adapted rather than invented from scratch.

### Gaps
- This estimate assumes the project's existing ECOD/COPOD code exposes per-dimension empirical CDF values (or equivalent) in a form reusable outside the final sum step; this was not verified against the actual project source (`src/esa_adb/models/...`) in this research pass, since the assignment scope was literature research, not codebase inspection. The report-writer or project team should confirm this before finalizing the hours estimate.
- No external benchmark exists for how long a "Gaussian copula on ~47 channels, walk-forward online" fit actually takes in wall-clock compute time (as opposed to developer hours) — this was out of scope for literature search and would need to be measured directly.

## Overall Feasibility Verdict (synthesis across all six questions)

**Verdict: worth implementing, as a clearly-scoped, novel-combination extension — not as replication of a named published method.**

- **What to cite it as:** Not a replication of a specific paper. The correct framing, directly supported by the findings above, is: "We extend the empirical-marginal-CDF framework of ECOD (Li et al. 2022) and COPOD (Li et al. 2020) — both of which assume independence across channels — with an explicit Gaussian-copula dependence step, following the probability-integral-transform-to-normal-scores construction standard in the copula literature (and implemented in an online/incremental setting by Zhao & Udell's AAAI-22 Gaussian-copula work), scoring the resulting vector via Mahalanobis distance using a correlation matrix fit robustly to contaminated data (informed by the robustness literature on rank-based vs. Pearson correlation estimators). ECOD's own authors (Li et al. 2022) explicitly name this class of extension — joint dependency modeling via copulas or graphical models — as their stated future work; to our knowledge, this specific empirical-marginal-to-Gaussian-copula combination has not been published or validated on real multivariate sensor/telemetry data." This framing is accurate, defensible, and turns the "novel combination" fact into a strength (a clearly motivated gap, not an arbitrary idea) for the paper's related-work/future-work sections.
- **Why it's worth doing:** (a) it directly tests the project's own core hypothesis — that explicit joint dependency modeling beats independent-then-combine scoring — on the exact empirical-CDF framework (ECOD/COPOD) that is currently the project's strongest non-ensemble model, generalizing the lesson already learned from the Kalman-Mahalanobis extension; (b) the component pieces (empirical CDFs, inverse-normal transform, Mahalanobis scoring) are all closed-form and cheap; (c) the one genuinely hard sub-problem (robust correlation estimation under contamination) already has a working precedent in this exact codebase (the Kalman fix), so it is not starting from zero; (d) the realistic effort (6-12 hours per Q6) fits comfortably within a "worth trying next" budget.
- **Why NOT to go further (vine/t-copula) right now:** Q4 shows that vine-copula structure selection and fitting is a combinatorial, offline-optimization-heavy problem that multiple independent sources flag as scaling poorly with channel count (47 channels here) — this would break the project's closed-form/CPU-feasible/few-hours constraint and should be explicitly deferred to future work, not attempted alongside the Gaussian-copula step. A t-copula (same correlation machinery plus one scalar degrees-of-freedom parameter) is a plausible, still-cheap "stretch" addition if the plain Gaussian copula shows evidence of missing joint-extreme (tail-dependent) anomalies, but should be scoped as a separate follow-up, not bundled into the initial implementation.
- **Key risk to flag explicitly in the paper:** rank-based correlation (Spearman or Gaussian-rank) is a meaningful but not unconditional robustness improvement over Pearson (Q3) — it is not a high-breakdown-point estimator on its own, so the implementation should still validate empirically (e.g., by comparing correlation-matrix stability with and without the project's known contaminating rows) rather than assuming rank-based estimation alone solves the contamination problem the way it solved the Kalman covariance blowup.
