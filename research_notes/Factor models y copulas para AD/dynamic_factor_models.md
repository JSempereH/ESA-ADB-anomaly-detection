# Dynamic Factor Models (DFM) for Multivariate, Contaminated, Online Anomaly Detection on Satellite Telemetry

## 1. Classic DFM formulation and anomaly-detection-framed applications

### Takeaway
The classic state-space DFM (`y_t = Λf_t + e_t`, `f_t = A f_{t-1} + u_t`) has a small but real anomaly-detection literature distinct from its econometric forecasting/nowcasting home turf. The core idea used across these papers is to split anomalies into **factor outliers** (hit many series simultaneously via the common component) and **idiosyncratic outliers** (hit one or a few series, i.e. a large residual `e_t` after removing the common factor part), detected by projecting observations onto robustly-estimated factor and idiosyncratic subspaces. However, essentially none of this literature targets spacecraft or even industrial sensor telemetry — it is almost entirely econometric (macro panels, financial panels), with one adjacent real-sensor exception (electrical distribution network monitoring).

### Cited Findings
- Stock & Watson and Geweke are the canonical foundational DFM state-space references (`y_t = Λf_t + e_t`, `f_t = A f_{t-1} + u_t`); confirmed as standard background in multiple secondary sources but not an anomaly-detection application itself — [MetricGate DFM docs](https://metricgate.com/docs/dynamic-factor-model/)
- Baragona & Battaglia, "Outliers in Dynamic Factor Models" (arXiv:0710.3676, posted 2007) explicitly add an outlier term to the observation equation, keeping the idiosyncratic disturbance assumed independent across series and time (any remaining cross-dependence should already be absorbed into the factors) — [arXiv:0710.3676](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0710.3676)
- Galeano & Peña, "Detecting Outliers in High-Dimensional Time Series by Dynamic Factor Models" (2024 book chapter, in Barigozzi, Hörmann & Paindaveine (eds.), *Recent Advances in Econometrics and Statistics*, Springer, DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-61853-6_19, pp. 361–383) — assumes outliers can strike either the latent factors (hitting many series at once = "factor outliers") or the idiosyncratic noise (hitting one/few series = "idiosyncratic outliers"); detects them by projecting observed series onto factor and idiosyncratic subspaces constructed from **robust** estimates of the loading matrix; validated on simulations and one real-data example — [IDEAS/RePEc listing](https://ideas.repec.org/h/spr/sprchp/978-3-031-61853-6_19.html), [UC3M listing](https://nicda.uc3m.es/publication/galeano/galeano-outliers-high-dimensional-time-series/)
- A FAPESP research-internship project description frames Galeano & Peña (2024) as a "robust estimation" response to outliers (as opposed to explicit outlier modeling, which needs prior detection), and describes a follow-up project evaluating how this detection method affects DFM estimation/forecasting accuracy — [FAPESP listing](https://bv.fapesp.br/en/bolsas/228561/URL)
- Kim, Song & Baek (2021, *Annals of the Institute of Statistical Mathematics* 73, p.821) propose a robust test for structural instability in DFMs based on a robust VAR fitted to the principal-component factors, specifically for data contaminated with outlying observations — [ISM AISM paper](https://www.ism.ac.jp/editsec/aism/73/0821.pdf)
- A real-sensor (non-econometric) precedent: a factor-model-residual approach for "Spatio-Temporal Correlation Analysis of Online Monitoring Data for Anomaly Detection and Location in Distribution Networks" finds that the residual covariance spectrum, after removing factors, did **not** fit the Marchenko–Pastur law regardless of how many factors were removed — residuals retained cross- and auto-correlation, which the authors then modeled explicitly rather than treating as white idiosyncratic noise — [arXiv:1810.08962](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.08962)
- `macroforecast` documentation notes residual autocorrelation at higher lags as a standard diagnostic that the idiosyncratic AR(1) assumption in a DFM is misspecified — [macroforecast docs](https://macroforecast.readthedocs.io/en/stable/_sources/encyclopedia/l3_5/axes/dfm_diagnostics.md.txt)

### Inferences
- The factor-vs-idiosyncratic split used in this literature maps directly onto the project's existing distinction between a global correlation-breakdown signal (what `PCAReconstructor`/block-Mahalanobis Kalman already capture) and a single-channel deviation signal (what per-channel Kalman already captures) — a DFM framed this way would not introduce a new detection *logic*, only a different way of estimating the loadings/factors used to compute that same idiosyncratic residual.
- The one real-sensor precedent (electrical distribution network, arXiv:1810.08962) is a cautionary finding: on real multivariate sensor data, idiosyncratic residuals from a factor model are **not** clean white noise as the textbook DFM assumes — there is leftover structure that would need explicit modeling (e.g., AR idiosyncratic terms, not simple iid Gaussian), adding estimation complexity before a closed-form threshold is trustworthy.

### Gaps
- No anomaly-detection-framed DFM paper validated specifically on spacecraft, industrial machine, or IoT sensor telemetry was found (see Q4 for the explicit, repeated search attempts). The Galeano & Peña (2024) and Baragona & Battaglia papers are both validated on macro/financial-style panels, not sensor telemetry, and full text of Galeano & Peña (2024) was not freely accessible to verify numerical results beyond the abstract.

## 2. Is there a genuinely online/incremental way to estimate both Λ and f_t without full offline EM/Kalman-smoother batch passes?

### Takeaway
No, not in the strict sense the question asks (recursively updating **both** loadings and factors online in closed form). Every mainstream DFM estimator either (a) estimates loadings once via a batch step (PCA/OLS) and then only filters the *factors* online by Kalman filtering with those loadings held fixed, or (b) uses full offline EM+Kalman-smoother iteration to jointly refine both. A genuinely sequential method that updates loadings and factors together does exist (PSMF, 2021) but relies on extended Kalman filtering (approximate/linearized, not exact closed-form) rather than a textbook closed-form recursion. For this project's protocol — refit loadings periodically per walk-forward window (closed-form, batch, like `IncrementalPCA` already is), then run an exact closed-form Kalman filter forward within the window with loadings fixed — is tractable and requires no EM/gradient descent.

### Cited Findings
- Doz, Giannone & Reichlin (2011), *Journal of Econometrics* 164(1), 188–205: a two-step estimator — step 1 estimates loadings/parameters by OLS on principal components (static, batch, closed form); step 2 recovers the factors via the Kalman smoother, which allows for factor dynamics and heteroskedastic idiosyncratic variance. Proves consistency as the cross-section (N) grows large. This is the estimator underlying the Giannone–Reichlin–Small nowcasting literature. The loadings are **not** updated recursively — only estimated once per batch — [search synthesis citing J. Econometrics 164(1) 188-205]
- Doz, Giannone & Reichlin (2012), *Review of Economics and Statistics* 94(4), 1014–1024: the quasi-maximum-likelihood (QML) version; factor estimates are consistent as both N and T → ∞ along any path, and the estimator is robust to misspecification of cross-sectional/serial correlation in the idiosyncratic components. Implementation uses the Kalman smoother jointly with **EM** — i.e., offline, iterative, not closed-form in one pass — [search synthesis citing REStat 94(4) 1014-1024]
- `sparseDFM` (R package, arXiv:2303.14125): uses the Kalman filter so that factor estimates are "continually updated as new observations become available," including a faster sequential (univariate) processing variant of the multivariate measurement update — but the loadings remain fixed at their first-step (batch) estimate; only the factors are updated online — [arXiv:2303.14125](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.14125)
- Koopman & Mesters (2014) treat the loading matrix itself, the dynamic factors, and the disturbances all as latent stochastic processes within an empirical-Bayes framework, reporting lower quadratic loss than standard ML — the most direct precedent for loadings that evolve as filtered states rather than fixed batch estimates, but it is a more complex Bayesian/empirical-Bayes procedure, not a simple closed-form recursion — [search synthesis, Koopman & Mesters 2014]
- Akyildiz, van den Burg, Damoulas & Steel, "Probabilistic Sequential Matrix Factorization" (AISTATS 2021, arXiv:1910.03906): factorizes a data matrix into a dictionary (loadings) and time-varying coefficients (factors) with Markovian dependencies, using **sequential approximate inference via an extended Kalman filter** — genuinely online for both loadings and factors. A robust variant (rPSMF) swaps in Student-t filters to handle model misspecification/contamination, and the authors state PSMF "can be used to robustify changepoint detection methods," though no anomaly-detection benchmark results were found in the accessible abstract — [arXiv:1910.03906](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03906)
- `dfms` (R package): estimates DFMs via EM, offering "BM" (Banbura & Modugno 2014, handles missing data/mixed frequency) and "DGR" (Doz-Giannone-Reichlin) variants — both are explicitly batch/offline EM estimators — [dfms docs](https://docs.ropensci.org/dfms/)
- `statsmodels.tsa.statespace.dynamic_factor_mq.DynamicFactorMQ` (Python): fitting (`.fit()`) is batch EM/ML. However, its results object exposes `.extend(new_data)`, which filters **only the new observations** using the already-estimated (fixed) parameters, much faster than refiltering the whole history, and `.append(new_data)` which appends new data to history with an option to refit or keep existing parameters — this is the practical Python mechanism closest to "online scoring with periodically-refit loadings" — [statsmodels docs: DynamicFactorMQResults.extend](https://www.statsmodels.org/dev/generated/statsmodels.tsa.statespace.dynamic_factor_mq.DynamicFactorMQResults.extend.html), [DynamicFactorMQResults.append](https://www.statsmodels.org/v0.14.4/generated/statsmodels.tsa.statespace.dynamic_factor_mq.DynamicFactorMQResults.append.html)

### Inferences
- For this project's walk-forward, no-look-ahead protocol, the practical and defensible design is: (1) re-estimate Λ (loadings) in closed form via `IncrementalPCA`/OLS at each walk-forward refit point — exactly analogous to how `PCAReconstructor` already works; (2) estimate the factor AR coefficient `A` by closed-form OLS of factor scores on their own lag (a tiny, cheap regression); (3) estimate idiosyncratic variances `R` (diagonal, can be made robust via MAD) and process noise `Q` in closed form from residuals; (4) run an **exact** closed-form Kalman filter forward through the window with (Λ, A, Q, R) fixed. This requires zero EM, zero gradient descent, and reuses the Kalman-filter machinery the project has already built for `KalmanBlockMultivariate`. This is effectively the Doz-Giannone-Reichlin two-step estimator, re-purposed for per-window refitting instead of one global batch fit.
- A fully "both-online" method (PSMF-style) would add real implementation complexity (extended Kalman filter linearization, Student-t robustification) for a benefit that is unproven for this specific use case (see Q5) — not a good effort/benefit trade given the project's preference for closed-form, no-gradient-descent methods.

### Gaps
- No benchmark or ablation was found quantifying how much accuracy is lost by using the "fixed loadings per window + online factor filtering" approximation versus a fully joint online loadings+factors method (e.g., PSMF) on any dataset, let alone sensor telemetry.

## 3. How many factors for ~47 correlated channels, and is there a principled online way to choose/update that number?

### Takeaway
No online/streaming method for choosing or adaptively updating the number of factors was found in the literature searched. Every tool and method located (R `onlinePCA`, `dfms`, `sparseDFM`, `statsmodels DynamicFactorMQ`) treats the number of factors/components as a fixed hyperparameter supplied by the user, typically chosen once via a static information criterion (e.g., Bai & Ng-style criteria, not directly surfaced in these searches) rather than estimated or adapted online.

### Cited Findings
- `onlinePCA` (R package) batch and incremental functions take the number of requested components `q` as an explicit, fixed argument — rank is a user-supplied hyperparameter, not something the algorithm estimates online — [onlinePCA CRAN manual](https://cran.r-universe.dev/onlinePCA/doc/manual.html)
- Cardot & Degras's review of online PCA algorithms (perturbation techniques, incremental SVD, stochastic optimization) likewise frames incremental SVD as producing "an approximate, reduced rank PCA," with the rank fixed in advance as a parameter rather than adaptively chosen — [arXiv:1511.03688](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.03688)
- No source located on eigenvalue-gap rules, sequential hypothesis tests on incoming eigenvalues, or information criteria updated online for rank selection. A targeted search ("number of factors selection online streaming factor analysis") returned only unrelated consumer/marketing "factor analysis" studies (OTT subscription choice surveys) and a title-only CS record with no methodological detail — [search result synthesis]

### Inferences
- Given the complete absence of an established online rank-selection method, the pragmatic approach for this project would be to fix the number of factors `k` per walk-forward refit using a static method evaluated on the current training window only (e.g., cumulative explained variance threshold, Bai & Ng IC, or simply matching whatever `PCAReconstructor` already uses for its retained-component count), and let `k` only change at refit boundaries — never inferred mid-window. This sidesteps the "no principled online method exists" gap rather than solving it.
- For 47 correlated housekeeping channels organized into subsystems, a reasonable a priori range (by analogy to the number of subsystem groups used in `SubsystemPCAEnsemble`, and to typical variance-retention choices in satellite telemetry PCA work) is low single digits to ~10; this is an inference from the project's own existing component counts, not from a literature-validated number for this dataset, since no DFM-specific factor-count guidance for spacecraft telemetry was found.

### Gaps
- No source gives a validated/recommended number of latent factors specifically for satellite or spacecraft housekeeping telemetry (47-channel or otherwise). This number would have to be determined empirically from this project's own training data (e.g., via the explained-variance curve `PCAReconstructor` already computes).

## 4. Has a DFM (or close relative: probabilistic PCA with time-varying factors, factor-analysis Kalman filter) been validated for anomaly detection on real industrial or spacecraft telemetry, 2023–2026?

### Takeaway
No. Despite five distinct, varied search attempts (general industrial 2024, spacecraft/satellite-specific, 2025-dated, and literature-review cross-checks), no paper was found that validates a true state-space DFM (or probabilistic-PCA-with-dynamics / factor-analysis Kalman filter) for anomaly detection on real spacecraft or industrial sensor telemetry in 2023–2026. The one close-but-partial match is static (not dynamic/state-space) factor analysis applied to satellite failure prediction. This is itself a meaningful negative finding for the project's related-work section: the DFM-for-AD application gap genuinely extends into spacecraft/industrial telemetry, not just into "general sensor data."

### Cited Findings
- A 2025 ITM Web of Conferences paper from Siberian researchers applies **factor analysis** (not a full dynamic state-space model) to low-orbit communication satellites, proposing to track how factor-loading models change over time to support failure prediction — framed as theoretical/conceptual, with the full text behind the publisher paywall and no quantitative anomaly-detection results surfaced in the abstract — [ITM Web of Conferences, 2025](https://www.itm-conferences.org/10.1051/itmconf/20257203008)
- Fejjari, Delavault, Camilleri & Valentino, "A Review of Anomaly Detection in Spacecraft Telemetry Data," *Applied Sciences* 15(10), article 5653 (2025) — a comprehensive literature survey of spacecraft telemetry AD methods reporting that GCN and TCN deep models reach up to ~94% precision; no DFM, factor-analysis, or Kalman-factor method appears flagged in the accessible summary of this survey, consistent with DFMs being absent from the spacecraft AD literature as of this 2025 review — [MDPI Applied Sciences 15(10):5653](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/10/5653)
- The closest real-sensor (non-spacecraft, non-econometric) precedent remains the electrical distribution-network monitoring paper (arXiv:1810.08962) discussed in Q1 — a genuine industrial multivariate sensor dataset, factor-residual-based anomaly detection AND location, but power-grid monitoring data, not spacecraft housekeeping telemetry — [arXiv:1810.08962](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.08962)
- A 2025 *Sensors* paper (STGLR) on spacecraft anomaly detection uses a dynamic **graph** module (not a factor model) to infer telemetry-variable relationships, reporting average F1 > 0.97 on two public spacecraft datasets — relevant as "nearest neighbor" methodology (also models cross-channel structure dynamically) but explicitly not a factor model — [PMC/Sensors STGLR](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11769452/)

### Inferences
- The absence of any DFM validation on spacecraft/industrial telemetry, combined with a 2025 survey paper (Fejjari et al.) covering the spacecraft AD space broadly and not surfacing factor models, is reasonably strong (though not 100% conclusive, since surveys can miss niche methods) evidence that DFM-for-AD is an unexploited niche specifically in this application domain — a genuine related-work gap worth stating explicitly in the paper, but also a sign that implementing one here would be exploring new ground without a template to validate expected performance against.

### Gaps
- Could not access full text of the 2025 ITM satellite factor-analysis paper or the Fejjari review to confirm with certainty that no factor-model baseline appears in either; this inference rests on abstract/summary-level evidence only.

## 5. Does a dynamic (AR/Kalman) factor model add anomaly-detection signal over static PCA, given PCAReconstructor already beats SubsystemPCAEnsemble?

### Takeaway
No direct evidence was found — in either direction — comparing a true state-space DFM against static PCA specifically for anomaly detection accuracy. The adjacent literature that does exist (static PCA vs. "dynamic PCA" in industrial process monitoring) uses "dynamic" to mean lag-augmented static PCA (stacking time-lagged copies of the observation vector before running ordinary PCA), not a Kalman-filtered AR factor process — a different and much cheaper notion of "dynamic" than the DFM formulation in this research question. Within that lag-augmented literature, "dynamic" variants do outperform static PCA on autocorrelated industrial data. But none of the anomaly-detection-framed DFM papers found (Baragona & Battaglia, Galeano & Peña) actually use the factor AR dynamics as part of their detection logic — their detection rule is a projection onto factor/idiosyncratic subspaces built from the loadings, which is available even from a static factor model. This suggests the AR/Kalman dynamics in a DFM are mainly a parameter-estimation efficiency device (better loadings/factor estimates in a high-dimensional panel via smoothing), not a mechanism that itself produces additional anomaly signal.

### Cited Findings
- A Tennessee-Eastman-style industrial benchmark study found that "DPCA-MD" (dynamic/lag-augmented PCA with Mahalanobis-distance statistic) achieved the highest fault detection rates on 17 of a set of faults, while plain static PCA and plain DPCA each performed well on only 4 — a reported ~5%-significance improvement of DPCA-MD over PCA/DPCA — [search synthesis, Tennessee Eastman DPCA-MD study]
- One 2022 industrial-monitoring study motivates dynamic methods by noting that "industrial process data generally show dynamic characteristics because certain sequences of process variables are autocorrelated," and combines static PCA for static variables with slow feature analysis for dynamic ones — i.e., even in this literature, dynamics-aware methods are typically hybridized with static PCA rather than replacing it outright — [search synthesis]
- A 2023 ENBIS conference talk comparing dynamics modeled explicitly in observed variables (DPCA with decorrelated residuals) versus implicitly in latent space (dynamic-inner CCA) found both approaches have limitations, including reduced sensitivity from "fault adaptation" and inability to handle integrating systems — a caution that dynamics-aware methods are not a uniform win — [search synthesis, ENBIS 2023]
- In the DFM-outlier-detection papers themselves (Q1), the detection mechanism (projection onto robustly-estimated factor/idiosyncratic subspaces) depends on the **loadings**, not on the factor AR transition matrix `A` — i.e., the published DFM anomaly-detection methods do not report the factor dynamics themselves as contributing to detection power; dynamics there serve estimation efficiency (via Kalman smoothing in high-dimensional panels), per Doz-Giannone-Reichlin's stated motivation — [search synthesis of Doz, Giannone & Reichlin 2011/2012, and Galeano & Peña 2024]

### Inferences
- Caution: "dynamic PCA beats static PCA" findings from process-monitoring literature do **not** transfer cleanly to this project's question, because that literature's "dynamic" (lag-stacking) is a different, cheaper mechanism than a Kalman/AR-filtered DFM; citing it as support for implementing a DFM here would overstate the evidence.
- Because the published DFM anomaly-detection papers locate their detection power in the loadings-based projection rather than in the factor transition dynamics, and because `PCAReconstructor` (itself effectively a static factor model with no factor dynamics) already outperforms `SubsystemPCAEnsemble`, the most defensible inference is: a DFM's dynamic/AR component is unlikely to add fresh anomaly-detection signal beyond what the loadings already provide; any marginal gain from building one here would more plausibly come from (a) explicit, heteroskedastic per-channel idiosyncratic noise modeling (`R` in the Kalman sense) giving better-calibrated per-channel residual scores than a single global PCA reconstruction-error norm, and (b) smoothing-based noise reduction of the factor estimate reducing false positives from measurement noise — both second-order refinements, not a new blind spot being closed (unlike the block-Mahalanobis Kalman extension, which closes a real blind spot: cross-channel correlation break invisible to independent per-channel filters).
- This is an inference, not a cited finding — no paper directly measures "static PCA AD score vs. same-loadings-but-with-AR-dynamics DFM AD score" head to head. Flagging this explicitly as the central evidentiary gap for the feasibility verdict.

### Gaps
- No ablation study isolating the contribution of factor AR dynamics (holding loadings fixed) to anomaly detection accuracy was found anywhere in the literature searched, on any dataset (macro, industrial, or spacecraft). This is the single most important missing piece of evidence for a confident verdict, and its absence is itself informative: it suggests this exact question has not been asked in the AD literature, i.e., building this would be closer to novel research than to replicating a known result.

## 6. Mature Python libraries for online/streaming DFM, and from-scratch implementation effort estimate

### Takeaway
No mature Python library offers genuinely online/incremental estimation of both loadings and factors for a DFM. `statsmodels.tsa.statespace.dynamic_factor_mq.DynamicFactorMQ` is batch EM/ML for fitting, but its results object's `.extend()`/`.append()` methods support efficient incremental *filtering* of new data against already-fixed parameters, which is a usable (if partial) building block. Building a closed-form, CPU-feasible, walk-forward-compatible DFM from scratch by reusing this project's existing `IncrementalPCA` (for loadings) and Kalman-filter infrastructure (for factors) is estimated at roughly 4–8 hours of implementation effort, since no EM or gradient-based fitting is required under the "fixed loadings per window" simplification.

### Cited Findings
- `statsmodels.tsa.statespace.dynamic_factor_mq.DynamicFactorMQ`: the primary Python implementation of DFMs (designed for large-scale, mixed-frequency nowcasting); `.fit()` is batch EM/ML; `.update()` only replaces a parameter vector, it does not accept new observations; `DynamicFactorMQResults.extend(new_data)` filters only the new data against existing fixed parameters (faster than refiltering from scratch, but returns results only for the new slice); `DynamicFactorMQResults.append(new_data)` appends new data to history with an option to refit or keep existing parameters — [statsmodels docs: DynamicFactorMQ.update](https://www.statsmodels.org/dev/generated/statsmodels.tsa.statespace.dynamic_factor_mq.DynamicFactorMQ.update.html), [DynamicFactorMQResults.extend](https://www.statsmodels.org/dev/generated/statsmodels.tsa.statespace.dynamic_factor_mq.DynamicFactorMQResults.extend.html)
- `dfms` (R, EM-based "BM"/"DGR" variants) and `sparseDFM` (R, Kalman filter + EM, sparse loadings) are both R-only, batch EM packages — no Python equivalent of either was found — [dfms CRAN readme](https://cran.hafro.is/web/packages/dfms/readme/README.html), [sparseDFM arXiv:2303.14125](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.14125)
- `dynaris` (Python, JAX-based) is described as covering composable state-space models including dynamic factor models, with automatic differentiation and GPU acceleration — but search results did not confirm whether its DFM fitting is EM-based, gradient-based (autodiff MLE), or supports true streaming updates; it appears to be a general toolkit rather than an off-the-shelf online-DFM solution — [PyPI dynaris listing](https://pypi.org/project/dynaris/)
- No Python package offering a documented "loop `.extend()` one observation at a time" workflow, or any package offering closed-form recursive *loadings* updates (not just factor updates), was found — [search synthesis]

### Inferences
- The practical, closed-form path for this project does not require any of the above libraries' EM fitting at all: it can be built directly on top of what the project already has. Concretely:
  1. Reuse `IncrementalPCA` (already used in `PCAReconstructor`) to get loadings `Λ` per walk-forward training window — closed form, already implemented.
  2. Add one closed-form OLS regression of the PCA factor scores on their own first lag to get the factor transition matrix `A` (diagonal or full, project's choice) — a few lines, no iteration needed.
  3. Estimate idiosyncratic covariance `R` (diagonal, optionally robust via MAD, analogous to what the Kalman filters already do) and process noise `Q` from in-window residuals — closed form.
  4. Run a standard linear-Gaussian Kalman filter forward through the window with (Λ, A, Q, R) fixed — exact closed-form predict/update recursion, directly reusing the project's existing `KalmanBlockMultivariate` filter mechanics, just swapping its per-subsystem (or global) observation-space state for a lower-dimensional latent-factor state.
  5. Anomaly score = idiosyncratic residual (`y_t − Λ f_t|t-1`), scored per-channel or via Mahalanobis distance using the filter's innovation covariance — same scoring pattern already used by the Kalman filters.
- Given this is almost entirely assembled from code the project has already written and validated (IncrementalPCA fitting loop from `PCAReconstructor`, Kalman filter recursion from `KalmanBlockMultivariate`), a realistic estimate is **4–8 hours**: ~1–2h for the factor-space Kalman state-space wiring, ~1h for the closed-form `A`/`Q`/`R` estimation, ~1–2h for walk-forward integration matching the existing `run_*` script patterns, ~1–2h for smoke-testing and threshold calibration (unsupervised, as required by protocol), plus buffer for debugging numerical issues (e.g., factor sign/scale ambiguity across refit windows, which also already needs handling in `PCAReconstructor`).

### Gaps
- No library benchmark or production case study was found establishing that this "fixed-loadings-per-window + closed-form Kalman filter" simplification performs comparably to a fully-refit EM-based DFM; this would need to be validated empirically within this project, not assumed from the literature.

## Overall Feasibility Verdict (synthesis across all six questions)

**Worth implementing in a few hours: yes, but as a cheap incremental experiment, not as a qualitatively new capability.**

- **What is genuinely tractable without violating the closed-form/no-gradient-descent preference**: fitting loadings via `IncrementalPCA` per walk-forward window (already proven), adding a closed-form AR(1) factor transition and a closed-form linear-Gaussian Kalman filter over the resulting low-dimensional factor state, exactly mirroring the estimation logic of Doz, Giannone & Reichlin's two-step estimator (OLS-on-PCA for loadings, then Kalman smoothing/filtering for factors) but applied per-window instead of globally. No EM, no iterative optimization, no gradient descent is required under this simplification.
- **What is NOT supported by any evidence found**: that the dynamic/AR part of a DFM adds anomaly-detection signal beyond what a static factor model (i.e., `PCAReconstructor`) already provides. No paper isolates this effect; the closest literature ("dynamic" PCA in process monitoring) uses a different, lag-augmentation notion of "dynamic" that is not comparable. The published DFM-for-outlier-detection papers (Baragona & Battaglia 2007; Galeano & Peña 2024) locate their detection power in loadings-based subspace projection, not in factor transition dynamics — consistent with the AR dynamics being primarily an estimation-efficiency device, not a detection-power device.
- **What has no validated precedent**: a DFM (or close relative: probabilistic PCA with time-varying factors, factor-analysis Kalman filter) applied to real spacecraft or industrial sensor telemetry for anomaly detection specifically, 2023–2026. This is a genuine, citable related-work gap (useful for the paper's related-work framing: "DFMs remain unexplored for spacecraft/industrial AD despite their natural fit to multivariate contaminated telemetry") but also means there is no external result to benchmark against or borrow confidence from.
- **What it would plausibly add, if anything, beyond `PCAReconstructor`**: an explicit, heteroskedastic per-channel idiosyncratic noise model (`R`) and filtered (noise-reduced) factor estimates, which could give better-calibrated per-channel residual scores than `PCAReconstructor`'s presumably more global reconstruction-error scoring — this is a second-order refinement in the same family as why the block-Mahalanobis Kalman extension beat the independent per-channel Kalman filter (closing a specific, nameable blind spot), but here the "blind spot" being closed (heteroskedastic per-channel residual scaling) is weaker and less clearly a blind spot specifically for PCA, since PCA reconstruction error can already be whitened per-channel without a full Kalman apparatus.
- **Recommended template if pursued**: Doz, Giannone & Reichlin (2011, *J. Econometrics* 164(1):188–205) two-step estimator (OLS-on-PCA loadings + Kalman smoothing of factors), re-purposed for per-walk-forward-window refitting, with the idiosyncratic-residual anomaly rule from Galeano & Peña (2024) (project onto idiosyncratic subspace via robust loadings, flag large idiosyncratic residual) as the scoring layer. This combination is closed-form end-to-end and maximally reuses existing project infrastructure (`IncrementalPCA` + Kalman filter).
- **Bottom line framing for the paper**: DFM is worth a cheap ablation precisely because it is nearly free to build from existing parts, but intellectual honesty requires stating that no literature evidence predicts it will beat static `PCAReconstructor` for anomaly detection specifically — the dynamic-factor literature's demonstrated advantages are in forecasting/nowcasting efficiency and in giving a principled language for factor-vs-idiosyncratic outlier attribution, not in a proven anomaly-detection accuracy lift over static factor models. If it is implemented and does not beat 0.089, that would itself be a citable negative result supporting "static PCA already captures most of the available linear-correlation signal for this task; dynamic factor evolution adds forecasting power, not anomaly-detection power" — directly answering the paper's own research question with project-generated evidence where the external literature is silent.
