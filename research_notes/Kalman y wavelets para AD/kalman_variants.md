# Kalman Filter Variants for Multivariate, Contaminated, Online Anomaly Detection on Real Sensor/Spacecraft Telemetry

Context for this note: the project already has a validated Harvey-style local-level/local-trend Kalman filter run **independently per channel** (no cross-channel covariance), with Q/R set in closed form from channel variance and first-difference variance, scored via per-row max standardized innovation. This note evaluates five candidate NEXT variants (robust, multivariate/correlated, adaptive, switching/IMM, nonlinear EKF/UKF) for feasibility, prior art specifically targeting anomaly detection (not generic state estimation), and validation on real multivariate industrial/spacecraft telemetry.

## 1. Robust Kalman filtering (Huber-loss / outlier-robust innovation handling)

### Takeaway
Well-established robust-Kalman theory exists (Huber-type "huberization" of the update, and generalized-maximum-likelihood-type (GM) estimators), with closed-form, non-iterative-per-step recursions available (RobKF's `AORKF_huber`) — this is feasible as a few-hours CPU implementation. However, essentially all of the concrete, citable work is motivated by **state-tracking robustness** (GPS car tracking, power-system state estimation, cyberattack-resilient estimation), not by **using the filter itself as an anomaly detector**; validation on real spacecraft/satellite multivariate telemetry specifically was not found.

### Cited Findings
- The CRAN package **RobKF** implements a Huberization-based additive-outlier robust Kalman filter based on Ruckdeschel, Spangl & Pupashenko (2014), *"Robust Kalman tracking and smoothing with propagating and non-propagating outliers,"* Statistical Papers 55(1), pp. 93–123 (also arXiv:1204.3358, posted 2012). The Huber threshold is a single tuning parameter (`h`, default 2) that clips/downweights the influence of large innovations or additive outliers rather than discarding them outright. — [RobKF CRAN manual](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/RobKF/refman/RobKF.html), [arXiv:1204.3358](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.3358)
- Ruckdeschel et al.'s method is built on earlier optimality results for distributionally-robust Kalman filtering (Ruckdeschel 2001, 2010b) and is formally derived for two distinct outlier models: "propagating" outliers (which corrupt the state going forward, i.e., innovation/process-side) and "non-propagating" (additive/observation-side) outliers — giving two different robust recursions. It is validated on a **GPS tracking problem from the car industry**, plus simulation studies against competitors on stylized outlier patterns — not on spacecraft or industrial sensor telemetry. — [arXiv:1204.3358](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.3358)
- The same RobKF package also implements CE-BASS (Fisch, Eckley & Fearnhead, 2020), which models **both** innovations and additive noise as potentially heavy-tailed (approximated via Student-t), using particle-sampled precision with conditionally-classical Kalman updates — more expressive but noticeably heavier computationally than the plain Huber variant (particle sampling vs. closed-form). — [RobKF CRAN manual](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/RobKF/refman/RobKF.html)
- Gandhi & Mili (2010), *"Robust Kalman Filter Based on a Generalized Maximum-Likelihood-Type Estimator,"* IEEE Transactions on Signal Processing, vol. 58, no. 5, pp. 2509–2520, is the standard non-Huber alternative: a GM-type estimator bounds the influence of observation, innovation, AND structural outliers simultaneously, and derives a corrected error-covariance matrix from the GM-estimator's influence function (so the covariance itself stays consistent under contamination, unlike naive clipping). — [VTechWorks dissertation abstract](https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/handle/10919/29902?show=full)
- Nonlinear/extended descendants of the GM approach exist: Zhao, Mili & Abdelhadi (IEEE PES General Meeting, 2017) propose GM-IEKF (robust iterated EKF) explicitly to suppress "observation and innovation outliers... induced by model parameter gross errors and cyber attacks" in power-system dynamic state estimation — i.e., motivated by anomalous/adversarial input robustness, though applied to power grids, not spacecraft. A GM-type UKF (GM-UKF) has similarly been applied to gene-regulatory-network state estimation under deviations from model assumptions. — [search summary citing Zhao/Mili/Abdelhadi 2017]
- Hajiyev and collaborators have an applied line of work combining innovation-based recursive measurement-noise-covariance estimation with a resulting "robust Kalman filter (RKF)" explicitly framed as **sensor/actuator fault detection**: they test both additive (constant bias) and multiplicative (noise-increase) fault types and use the filter's innovation statistics (often chi-square gated) to both detect and compensate for the fault. This is the closest found prior art to "robust KF as anomaly detector," but it is in flight-control/aircraft sensor fusion settings (ITU research group), not spacecraft telemetry. — [ITU research group publications on robust/adaptive KF for sensor fault diagnosis](https://research.itu.edu.tr/en/publications/robust-kalman-filter-with-recursive-measurement-noise-covariance-/), [PHM Society paper](https://papers.phmsociety.org/index.php/ijphm/article/view/4204)
- A 2024 ICML paper, Duran-Martin et al., *"Outlier-robust Kalman Filtering through Generalised Bayes,"* proposes a generalized-Bayesian robust filter that the authors report matches or outperforms variational-Bayes-based robust filters "at a much lower computational cost" — relevant as a potentially cheaper modern alternative to the classical Huber/GM machinery, though again framed as general robust state estimation, not spacecraft anomaly detection specifically. — [ICML 2024 proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/duran-martin24a.html)

### Inferences
- Mechanistically, Huber/GM-robust KF updates are a strict generalization of a chi-square-gated standard KF (which the project likely already implicitly resembles if any gating exists): instead of a hard threshold that either fully accepts or fully rejects an observation, the Kalman gain for that channel/step is continuously down-weighted as the normalized innovation grows. This is attractive specifically BECAUSE the data are known-contaminated and labels are never available even for threshold calibration — a robust filter's self-tuned downweighting could reduce state corruption from the genuine anomalies that are scattered throughout the "normal" walk-forward history, which a plain closed-form per-channel local-trend filter has no defense against.
- However: the core thing that would make this "beat" the already-implemented filter is whether avoiding state corruption from contamination actually changes the *detection* scores and not just the state track — and no paper found reports that comparison directly on relevant real telemetry. There's a non-trivial double-edged risk: outlier-downweighting could also suppress the very innovation spikes used as the anomaly score, blunting sensitivity rather than sharpening it, if the robustification constant is not tuned very carefully against the no-labels constraint.

### Gaps
- No paper found that validates a Huber-type or GM-type robust Kalman filter as an anomaly-detection method on real multivariate spacecraft/satellite or industrial sensor telemetry specifically (closest is aircraft sensor/actuator fault detection, and power-grid cyberattack detection — both real industrial settings, but not spacecraft, and not validated against the metrics this project uses, e.g. event-wise F0.5).
- Could not retrieve the exact closed-form update equations for `AORKF_huber` (the RobKF package vignette/manual) in this session; the described behavior (clip influence via Huber threshold `h`) is confirmed from the package description but the precise weighting formula was not directly extracted.

## 2. Multivariate/correlated Kalman filtering (joint state-space model across all channels)

### Takeaway
A joint multivariate Kalman filter with a full/structured process+observation covariance is the theoretically most natural way to catch anomalies that break normal cross-channel correlation (something independent per-channel filters structurally cannot see), and tractable structured approximations (block-diagonal / banded / factored covariance) exist in the control-theory literature that avoid the naive O(n³) cost — but no paper was found that applies a joint-covariance Kalman filter specifically for anomaly detection on real multivariate spacecraft telemetry; real-telemetry multivariate anomaly detection work that models cross-channel correlation on SMAP/MSL-type data instead uses deep learning (graph attention, LSTM-based), not a joint Kalman filter.

### Cited Findings
- **Computational tractability (the key blocker):** Pogorelyuk, Rowley & Kasdin propose a filter for multiple systems coupled via low-dimensional stochastic input that **drops the off-diagonal blocks of the correlation matrix in the update step**, making the covariance-propagation cost scale **linearly** in the number of coupled subsystems instead of cubically for the full joint filter — i.e., a practical block-diagonal/structured approach exists and is the direct analogue of "per-subsystem block-diagonal covariance" asked about. — [arXiv:1911.10443, "An Efficient Approximation of the Kalman Filter for Multiple Systems Coupled via Low-Dimensional Stochastic Input"](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.10443)
- Riedel studies near-block-diagonal transition/observation matrices and shows the resulting sub-optimal filter/smoother remain positive semidefinite with better numerical properties than naively dropping off-diagonal terms in an arbitrary system. — [arXiv:1803.05083, "Block Diagonally Dominant Positive Definite Sub-optimal Filters and Smoothers"](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1803.05083)
- Factored/square-root forms (UD decomposition, P = UDUᵀ) are a standard, closed-form, numerically stable way to represent and update a full joint covariance without cubic blow-up in practice for moderate state dimensions, and Suzdaleva's LDL-factorized KF work applies this to traffic-network state estimation (a genuinely multivariate real-world system, though not anomaly detection). — [alphaxiv overview of UD filter](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2203.06105v2), [UTIA factorized KF paper](https://webarchiv.lib.cas.cz/wayback/20091030170227/http://as.utia.cas.cz/files/phdws06/fullpaper_file_34.pdf)
- Chandrasekar & Bernstein note the standard KF's covariance-propagation cost is O(n³) and that **banded covariance propagation** reduces this for large-scale data assimilation settings — directly relevant to whether 47–58 channels is tractable (it is a small-to-moderate n by the standards of the data-assimilation literature, where n can be in the thousands-to-millions; Kitanidis's "compressed state Kalman filter" targets millions of states for reservoir monitoring using a rank-N orthogonal-basis covariance approximation). — [ACC06 paper, Jaganath/Bernstein](https://websites.umich.edu/~dsbaero/library/ConferencePapers/ACC06Papers/JaganathBandedACC06.pdf), [Kitanidis compressed-state KF](https://repository.usfca.edu/envs/21)
- No source found confirms that any of these structured/factored covariance approximations have been combined with an **anomaly-detection** innovation test and validated — this gap was explicit in search results: "None of the results addressed online anomaly detection directly... you would need to check whether that approximation keeps the innovation covariance accurate enough for the test."
- Real multivariate spacecraft-telemetry anomaly detection work that explicitly models cross-channel correlation uses non-Kalman architectures: (a) Liu, Tian, Kang & Wan (2023), *"Spacecraft Anomaly Detection with Attention Temporal Convolution Networks"* (arXiv:2303.06879) uses dynamic graph attention over channels plus stacked dilated causal TCNs, validated on NASA SMAP (P/R/F1 = 0.954/0.902/0.927) and MSL (0.942/0.982/0.961), reporting 2.3%/5.0% F1 gains over the prior graph-attention baseline (GTA/MTAD-GAT family) — confirming that modeling cross-channel correlation does measurably help on exactly this class of spacecraft dataset, just not via a Kalman filter. — [ar5iv:2303.06879](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.06879)
- A hierarchical-clustering approach for Chinese satellite telemetry (Beijing Aerospace Flight Control Center data) uses the maximum information coefficient to group correlated telemetry parameters into subspaces, then runs per-subspace LSTM detection — structurally the same "group then model jointly" idea the block-diagonal Kalman approach would implement, but again not a Kalman filter. — [search summary, hierarchical agglomerative clustering satellite anomaly paper]
- A sparse-representation/dictionary-learning method (Pilastre et al.) explicitly targets **contextual anomalies that only show up across channels** in mixed continuous/discrete multivariate telemetry — direct confirmation that the failure mode described in the question (anomalies that break normal cross-channel relationships, invisible to independent per-channel models) is a recognized, named problem class in the telemetry anomaly-detection literature. — [HAL preprint, "Anomaly detection in mixed telemetry data using a sparse representation and dictionary learning"](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-02466360)
- Maleki Sadr & Qaraqe (2024), *"Uncompromised Accuracy: Fast and Reliable Multivariate Anomaly Detection for Satellite Signals,"* IEEE Transactions on Aerospace and Electronic Systems, vol. 61, no. 2, pp. 1505–1517 — explicitly frames nonlinear Kalman variants (EKF/UKF) as fast-but-less-accurate, and deep models as accurate-but-costly, then proposes a "selective points" method that fuses/selects between them by comparing estimated covariances, reporting accuracy-to-time-ratio (ATR) gains of ≥30% (SMAP) and ≥33% (MSL rover) over the best prior method. Keywords include "interacting multiple models" — this paper is relevant to both Q2 (multivariate) and Q4 (switching/IMM) below; full methodological detail (exactly how covariance-based model selection works, whether it's a true joint multivariate state) could not be confirmed, only abstract/metadata. — [HBKU repository record](https://elmi.hbku.edu.qa/en/publications/uncompromised-accuracy-fast-and-reliable-multivariate-anomaly-det-2/)

### Inferences
- 47–58 channels is small enough that a naive full joint Kalman filter (state dimension ~2×47=94 for a joint local-level+trend model) is very likely tractable in closed form even without block-diagonal tricks: O(n³) with n≈100 is on the order of 10⁶ flops per update step, trivial for CPU at 18–30s cadence. The "is this tractable" question the user raises is probably not a real blocker at this specific channel count — the harder problem is **not compute cost but estimating a reliable 47×47 (or 94×94) covariance matrix from contaminated, non-stationary, walk-forward data without overfitting it to a changing correlation structure** (mission phases, operational-mode changes) using only closed-form/no-MLE estimation. This estimation-robustness problem, not the O(n³) cost, is the real risk for a few-hours implementation.
- A pragmatic structured middle ground (per-subsystem block-diagonal covariance, akin to Pogorelyuk et al.) is a sensible closed-form-feasible design: partition channels into physically meaningful subsystems (e.g., thermal, power, attitude/ADCS) and model only within-block correlation, keeping cost linear in the number of blocks and making the covariance estimate much more sample-efficient than a full 47×47 matrix — but no implemented or published example of this applied to anomaly detection specifically was found, so this would be a genuinely novel contribution if pursued, not a replication of prior art.
- Given that the deep correlation-modeling literature (TCN+graph-attention, LSTM-on-correlated-subspaces, sparse dictionary learning) consistently reports gains from modeling cross-channel relationships on this exact class of data (SMAP/MSL, Chinese satellite telemetry), it is plausible a joint/structured Kalman filter could also pick up genuine signal the per-channel filter misses — but because no prior work isolates "Kalman-specific" joint-covariance gains from "any model that captures correlation" gains, there's no direct evidence the *Kalman* mechanism specifically (vs. just correlation-awareness in general) is what drives those reported improvements.

### Gaps
- No paper found that implements a joint multivariate (or structured block-diagonal) Kalman filter specifically as an anomaly-detection scorer and validates it on real multivariate spacecraft or industrial telemetry — this entire combination (Kalman + joint covariance + AD + real telemetry) appears to be an open gap in the literature, not a solved/replicated problem.
- Could not confirm whether the Maleki Sadr & Qaraqe (2024) "selective points" method actually uses a true joint multivariate state-space model, or just runs independent per-channel/per-sensor EKF/UKF models in parallel and fuses their outputs after the fact (the latter would NOT answer the cross-channel-correlation question at all) — full text was not accessible in this session, only abstract/metadata.

## 3. Adaptive Kalman filtering (online Q/R estimation, not fixed from channel variance)

### Takeaway
Multiple genuinely online, non-MLE, non-batch-EM adaptive-noise-covariance algorithms exist and are implementation-feasible in a few hours — the classical innovation-correlation method (Mehra 1970/1972) and its lightweight descendant the Sage-Husa filter are the standard lightweight choices — but Sage-Husa is well-documented to be unstable (cannot jointly estimate Q and R without risking divergence) and the clearest anomaly-detection-specific applications found are in non-spacecraft domains (AUV navigation, aircraft sensor fault diagnosis); a more modern lightweight and more stable alternative is innovation-based covariance matching combined with chi-square gating, which several real fault-diagnosis papers use specifically to catch anomalies rather than just improve tracking.

### Cited Findings
- Mehra (1970, *IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control*, title recalled as "On the identification of variances and adaptive Kalman filtering," not independently verified in this session) is the foundational innovation-correlation method: it exploits the fact that a necessary and sufficient condition for KF optimality is that the innovation sequence be white and Gaussian, and estimates noise covariances from the auto/cross-correlation of the innovations — this is the origin of essentially all lightweight online adaptive-Q/R methods used since. — [search summary of citations to Mehra 1970/1972]
- A PMC/arXiv paper, *"On the Identification of Noise Covariances and Adaptive Kalman Filtering: A New Look at a 50 Year-Old Problem,"* derives rank conditions on a matrix of weighted innovation auto/cross-covariances under which Q and R are even identifiable at all, and proposes a gradient-based successive-approximation estimator with adaptive step sizes that keeps the estimated covariances symmetric positive-definite — directly relevant as a principled, closed-form-adjacent (non-MLE, non-batch-EM, iterative-but-per-step) online estimator. — [NCBI PMC8638515](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8638515/)
- The **Sage-Husa adaptive Kalman filter** is the most commonly cited "lightweight online" adaptive-noise-covariance method in applied fault-diagnosis/navigation literature. A 1998 *Journal of Aeronautics* paper is cited as showing Sage-Husa **cannot estimate Q and R simultaneously** without risking filter divergence — an important caveat for anyone considering it as a drop-in replacement for the fixed-variance Q/R currently used. — [search summary citing 1998 Journal of Aeronautics critique]
- A 2022 *Sensors* paper on AUV localization combines Sage-Husa with a chi-square test on innovations specifically to flag "measurement anomalies": above-threshold statistics trigger a switch to suboptimal MAP estimation with weighted exponential fading memory, and the measurement-noise covariance is adjusted online in response. The authors report this "significantly reduced the average RMS and standard deviation of localization errors when measurement anomalies were present" — i.e., a concrete, published example of adaptive-Q/R + anomaly flagging improving a real multivariate sensor-fusion system, though on AUV navigation, not spacecraft telemetry. — [DOAJ/Sensors 2022](https://doaj.org/article/68832c67544b4ecc8edd63e64ab620ff)
- A robust-adaptive-UKF line of work uses a **gated update**: an online fault-detection test decides WHETHER the noise covariance needs updating at all before applying innovation-based (process noise) and residual-based (measurement noise) corrections, blended with previous estimates via a weighting factor — this gating is a sensible safeguard against adaptive covariance estimation being corrupted by the contamination itself (directly relevant given the project's "zero labels, contaminated data" constraint). — [NCBI PMC5876731](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5876731/)
- An MDPI paper on **Online Adaptive Kalman Filtering (OAKF) for wireless sensor networks** dynamically adjusts both the filter parameters AND the anomaly-detection threshold together in response to live streaming data, explicitly designed for resource-constrained sensor nodes — the closest found match to "lightweight, online, anomaly-detection-specific, walk-forward-compatible" adaptive KF, though on generic WSN data rather than spacecraft telemetry specifically. — [MDPI 24(15):5046](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/24/15/5046)
- Hajiyev's group (ITU) explicitly frames innovation-based recursive measurement-noise-covariance estimation as producing a "robust Kalman filter (RKF)" used for **sensor/actuator fault detection** in flight-control/aircraft settings, testing additive (bias) and multiplicative (noise-increase) faults and using the resulting adapted covariance plus chi-square-type statistics to distinguish and diagnose the two fault types. — [ITU research publications on recursive measurement-noise-covariance RKF](https://research.itu.edu.tr/en/publications/robust-kalman-filter-with-recursive-measurement-noise-covariance-/)
- Variational-Bayes adaptive KF (VB-AKF) is the more modern and more theoretically grounded alternative to Sage-Husa: Särkkä & Hartikainen extend VB-AKF (originally measurement-noise-only) to estimate the **full process-noise covariance matrix** in nonlinear state-space models using Gaussian filtering (unscented/cubature/Gauss-Hermite/Taylor), reducing posterior inference to an iterative-but-closed-form (inverse-Wishart conjugate-prior-based) optimization rather than MLE/gradient descent over a batch — genuinely online, per-step, and not requiring offline EM. — [ar5iv:1302.0681](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1302.0681)
- Ma et al. (FUSION 2018) and Liu et al. (IET Radar, Sonar & Navigation, 2023) extend VB-AKF specifically for **time-varying / mismatched process-noise covariance**, using an inverse-Wishart latent variable updated per step — validated on target tracking (not spacecraft telemetry), but directly addresses the "online, not batch-EM" requirement. — [search summary]

### Inferences
- Of the five variants, adaptive Q/R estimation is the one with the clearest, most directly "lightweight and online" prior art (Mehra/innovation-correlation, Sage-Husa, VB-AKF), and the gating-based robust-adaptive-UKF pattern (only adapt when NOT flagged as faulty) is a well-motivated design for the project's fully-unsupervised, contaminated-data constraint: it would prevent the real anomalies scattered through walk-forward history from "teaching" the filter to treat anomalous variance as normal — a real structural risk if naive adaptive Q/R were applied blindly to contaminated historical data without such gating.
- However, the current fixed-Q/R-from-channel-variance approach is already, in effect, a crude one-shot "adaptive" estimate (it IS estimated from the data, just not updated online). The marginal benefit of a true online adaptive scheme would most likely show up as improved tracking through slow regime drift (e.g., gradual sensor degradation, seasonal/thermal cycles) rather than as sharper anomaly flags per se — and no source found demonstrates the detection-metric-level (vs. tracking-RMSE-level) benefit specifically. This mirrors a pattern across all the robust/adaptive literature found: nearly all reported gains are in state-tracking-accuracy terms, not anomaly-detection-F-score terms.

### Gaps
- No paper found that applies online adaptive Q/R estimation (Sage-Husa, Mehra-style, or VB-AKF) to spacecraft/satellite telemetry anomaly detection specifically and reports detection-metric (precision/recall/F-score) results — all concrete anomaly-flagging validations found are in AUV navigation or aircraft sensor fault diagnosis.
- Could not confirm whether any adaptive-Q/R method has been tested under a walk-forward (strictly causal, refit-as-you-go) protocol matching this project's exact evaluation setup, as opposed to a single offline-tuned run.

## 4. Switching/regime-based Kalman filtering (IMM or similar)

### Takeaway
IMM-based Kalman filtering for spacecraft is a mature, decades-old line of work, but almost entirely for **fault diagnosis/isolation in the attitude-control and actuator/sensor domain** (each "mode" = a specific fault hypothesis, not an operational regime like safe-mode vs. nominal-mode); the one 2024 paper that explicitly frames IMM-style multi-model combination as telemetry anomaly detection reports strong accuracy-to-time gains on real spacecraft/rover datasets (SMAP, MSL) but full methodological detail was not retrievable, and no paper was found validating IMM specifically for the "distinguish normal mode transition from anomaly" framing the question asks about.

### Cited Findings
- Tudoroiu & Khorasani (2007), *"Satellite Fault Diagnosis Using a Bank of Interacting Kalman Filters,"* IEEE Transactions on Aerospace and Electronic Systems, vol. 43, no. 4, pp. 1334–1350 — the standard foundational reference for IMM-style satellite fault diagnosis: runs a bank of Kalman filters, each corresponding to a specific fault/system mode, with the IMM's mixing step combining per-mode estimates in proportion to each mode's posterior likelihood. Full text/equations not retrieved in this session, only bibliographic confirmation. — [ISIJ bibliographic record](https://isij.eu/article/satellite-fault-diagnosis-using-bank-interacting-kalman-filters)
- Rapoport & Oshman (2007), IEEE TAES, directly critique standard IMM for fault-tolerant estimation: it "does not utilize the independence of the measurement errors in different channels, nor does it exploit the fact that the process noise is of low intensity" — their work targets fixing exactly these inefficiencies, implying plain off-the-shelf IMM leaves real performance on the table for multichannel systems like spacecraft, which is a relevant caveat for a few-hours implementation (the "obvious" IMM design is known-suboptimal for this problem shape). — [Oshman group PDF](https://oshman.technion.ac.il/files/2016/04/Rapoport_Oshman_TAES_V43_N2_Apr07.pdf)
- A 2010 IAC conference paper applies IMM combined with an **unscented** Kalman filter to satellite failure-related state estimation, reporting that "the IMM estimation introduces the interaction among single-model-based filters, and therefore it leads to significantly improved performance" versus non-interacting multi-model banks — confirms the "interacting" mixing step (vs. a naive independent multi-model bank) is the mechanism that matters, not just having multiple models. — [IAC-10/C1/6/6191 archive](https://iafastro.directory/iac/archive/browse/IAC-10/C1/6/6191/)
- A 2010 Korean GNSS Workshop paper combines IMM with **federated** Kalman filters for sensor/actuator fault detection and isolation in a GPS-based satellite attitude-control system — another real (if simulation-validated) spacecraft application, decentralized/federated design being relevant to the project's per-channel-filter starting point (federated = local filters + a fusion step, structurally similar to "keep per-channel filters but add a combination layer").
- Maleki Sadr & Qaraqe (2024), IEEE TAES vol. 61 no. 2 — the most directly relevant modern paper: keywords explicitly list "interacting multiple models (IMM)" alongside Kalman filtering and LSTM, proposes model-fusion/selection by comparing estimated covariances to maximize an accuracy-to-time ratio, and reports ≥30%/≥33% ATR gains over the best prior method on SMAP and MSL rover data respectively — i.e., real satellite-signal and real Mars-rover telemetry, which is the closest found validation of an IMM-flavored approach to this project's actual data modality. Full algorithmic detail (how exactly IMM-style mixing enters the "selective points" method) not retrievable in this session. — [HBKU repository](https://elmi.hbku.edu.qa/en/publications/uncompromised-accuracy-fast-and-reliable-multivariate-anomaly-det-2/)
- A 2021 American Control Conference paper (McMaster group) uses IMM with a UKF for adaptive spacecraft controller design, validated only in Monte Carlo simulation, not real telemetry — included for completeness but weaker evidentiary value (simulation only, control design rather than anomaly detection).

### Inferences
- The dominant use of IMM in the spacecraft literature is fault/actuator diagnosis where each filter mode corresponds to a specific hypothesized fault, which is a different problem framing than "nominal operational mode A vs. nominal operational mode B vs. anomaly" the question poses — adapting IMM to the latter framing (multiple NORMAL regime models, with anomaly = none of the modes fit well / abnormal mode-posterior behavior) would be a reasonable but not directly-precedented design; this is closer to the "regime-switching + residual-based anomaly flag" idea than to any single cited paper's exact setup.
- A full from-scratch IMM implementation (bank of N interacting filters + mixing probabilities + mode likelihoods) is more implementation complexity than the other variants for a "few-hours" budget — closed-form and non-iterative in principle, but has more moving parts (mode transition matrix design, which itself is unsupervised/unlabeled and would need to be learned or hand-set) than robust-KF or adaptive-Q/R, making it the least "few-hours-feasible" of the three more tractable variants (robust, adaptive, switching), though still more feasible than nonlinear filtering if the state model stays linear-Gaussian per mode.
- Given that Mission 2 is described as 47 channels at 18s resolution (and ESA-ADB missions are known in the literature to include distinct telemetry channel types across subsystems, with likely-distinct operating modes e.g. safe mode/science mode), a lightweight 2-3 mode IMM (e.g., "quiescent" vs. "active/maneuvering" mode) is plausible as a strict superset of the current single-mode local-trend filter and might reduce false positives at legitimate mode transitions — but no paper provides direct evidence this would improve the specific event-wise F0.5 metric the project optimizes for, and the added complexity/hyperparameter surface (transition probabilities, number of modes, per-mode noise) is a real risk in a zero-label setting where none of these can be calibrated against ground truth.

### Gaps
- No paper found that validates IMM/switching Kalman filtering for anomaly detection (as opposed to fault-mode diagnosis or control) on real ESA-style or NASA-style ground-segment spacecraft housekeeping telemetry with an event-wise or precision/recall-style metric.
- Could not retrieve full methodological or quantitative detail for Maleki Sadr & Qaraqe (2024) beyond the abstract/metadata (IEEE Xplore paywall) — this is the single most relevant paper found for this question and ought to be obtained in full if this variant is pursued further.

## 5. Unscented/Extended Kalman Filter (nonlinear state-space dynamics)

### Takeaway
EKF/UKF applications to real spacecraft telemetry-based fault/anomaly monitoring exist and include at least one validated-on-real-on-orbit-telemetry case (Chinese star-sensor fault detection), but the bulk of the literature uses EKF/UKF for attitude/actuator **state/fault estimation with a physically-motivated nonlinear dynamics model** (attitude kinematics, orbital mechanics) rather than for generic housekeeping-channel anomaly detection; for the kind of scalar housekeeping channels (temperatures, voltages, currents) implied by "47 channels" with no stated physical nonlinear dynamics model, there is no evidence found that nonlinear filtering would outperform the already-implemented linear local-trend model, and one directly relevant 2024 paper explicitly states nonlinear Kalman variants "often compromise accuracy" relative to alternatives.

### Cited Findings
- A China Academy of Space Technology study performs star-sensor fault detection when gyro telemetry is unavailable: it augments the UKF state with angular rate, compares filter estimates against measured values to flag faults, simulates both an abrupt constant fault and a noise-increase fault, AND explicitly validates the method on **real on-orbit telemetry data** — this is the clearest found instance of UKF-based anomaly/fault detection validated on genuine spacecraft telemetry (not simulation only). — [jnwpu.org Chinese-language paper with English abstract](https://www.jnwpu.org/articles/jnwpu/abs/2019/06/jnwpu2019376p1158/jnwpu2019376p1158.html)
- Xiong, Chan & Zhang (IFAC 2005) derive UKF convergence conditions and use a statistical local approach on filter residuals to detect changes, applied to satellite attitude-sensor fault detection — an early, foundational residual-based UKF fault-detection reference, though the application (attitude sensors with known nonlinear kinematics) is a case where nonlinearity is physically real and well-motivated, unlike generic scalar housekeeping channels. — [NTNU IFAC2005 proceedings](https://skoge.folk.ntnu.no/prost/proceedings/ifac2005/Papers/Paper1635.html)
- Federated/decentralized UKF schemes (Korean IAC 2009 papers) are used for fault-tolerant attitude determination and fault detection/isolation via sensitivity factors or threshold-based residual tests with pre-filtering to suppress false alarms from sudden noise spikes — relevant design pattern (pre-filtering against false alarms) regardless of linear/nonlinear choice.
- Chen et al. (IET Control Theory & Applications, 2018) propose a two-stage UKF for simultaneous actuator+sensor fault **estimation** (not just detection) in spacecraft attitude control, without state augmentation or linearization needed — more fault-estimation-for-control-recovery than telemetry-anomaly-flagging.
- A 2025 arXiv preprint (Chidambaram, Hilbert & Silva) compares EKF, UKF, and particle filters for fault-tolerant attitude estimation on a large LEO satellite, and separately surveys techniques built on these filters for FDIR (fault detection, isolation, recovery) — a useful recent comparative reference, though not peer-reviewed and the comparison is for attitude estimation accuracy/robustness, not a report of anomaly-detection F-scores on housekeeping telemetry. — [arXiv:2506.21016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21016v1)
- Directly on point for the "is nonlinearity worth it" question: Maleki Sadr & Qaraqe (2024) state plainly that "nonlinear Kalman filter variants (EKF, UKF) are known for their efficient time complexity but often compromise accuracy" relative to their proposed model-fusion method and relative to deep-learning alternatives, in the context of SMAP/MSL-type satellite signal anomaly detection — i.e., a paper working on structurally similar data to this project's benchmark explicitly frames UKF/EKF nonlinearity as an accuracy trade-off, not a clear win, for this exact task. — [HBKU repository record](https://elmi.hbku.edu.qa/en/publications/uncompromised-accuracy-fast-and-reliable-multivariate-anomaly-det-2/)
- A Tufts SSC 2022 paper combining Kalman filters, autoencoders, and causality algorithms for cross-subsystem spacecraft diagnosis notes that "the most widely used fault-handling approach onboard satellites employs basic limit checking on telemetry values" — useful context establishing that even simple, non-Kalman limit-checking is the actual operational baseline in practice, underscoring that model sophistication (linear vs. nonlinear Kalman) is a second-order concern relative to simply having ANY dynamic/model-based detector at all. — [Tufts SSC2022 paper](https://mulip.cs.tufts.edu/papers/gizzi_ssc_2022.pdf)

### Inferences
- The housekeeping channels implied by ESA-ADB Mission 1/2 (temperatures, voltages, currents, etc. at 18–30s cadence) do not have an obvious, well-characterized nonlinear physical dynamics model the way attitude/orbital quantities do (quaternion kinematics, orbital mechanics are inherently nonlinear and that's WHY the EKF/UKF literature concentrates there) — the found evidence supports treating UKF/EKF as motivated specifically by nonlinear physical dynamics (attitude, orbit, gyro/star-sensor cross-checks), not by generic housekeeping-signal anomaly detection, which is consistent with the Harvey local-trend linear model already being a reasonable and sufficient choice for this project's channel types.
- Given a real paper working on directly comparable data (SMAP/MSL satellite signals) states EKF/UKF "often compromise accuracy," and no paper was found reporting an anomaly-detection-metric improvement from switching a per-channel housekeeping filter from linear to nonlinear, this is the variant least likely among the five to beat the existing implementation, and the added implementation complexity (linearization or sigma-point propagation per channel, tuning of nonlinearity assumptions with zero labels) is hard to justify without a specific physical nonlinearity hypothesis for the channels in question.

### Gaps
- No paper found applying EKF/UKF to generic (non-attitude, non-orbital) spacecraft housekeeping telemetry anomaly detection with a quantified precision/recall/F-score comparison against a linear alternative — the comparison the project would need (linear local-trend vs. nonlinear local-trend on the SAME housekeeping channels) does not appear to exist in the literature.
- Could not access full text of the star-sensor on-orbit validation paper (Chinese-language journal) to confirm quantitative fault-detection performance figures; only the abstract-level description (fault types tested, real on-orbit validation claim) was available.

## Overall Verdict Summary (feasibility + likely payoff)

| Variant | Few-hours CPU closed-form feasibility | Anomaly-detection-specific prior art found | Validated on real multivariate spacecraft/industrial telemetry | Likely to beat existing per-channel linear filter |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Robust (Huber/GM) | High — RobKF-style Huberization is a drop-in modification of the update step | Some (aircraft sensor/actuator fault diagnosis, cyberattack-resilient power-grid GM-IEKF) | Partial (aircraft, power grid — not spacecraft) | Plausible but unproven; main risk is downweighting also suppresses the anomaly signal itself |
| 2. Multivariate/correlated | Medium — full joint filter is cheap at n≈100 states, but reliable covariance estimation from contaminated walk-forward data without MLE is the hard part; block-diagonal/structured variant is the practical path | None found specifically for Kalman; strong non-Kalman precedent (graph attention, LSTM-on-correlated-subspaces) that correlation-awareness helps on this exact data class | No (gap for Kalman specifically; yes for non-Kalman correlation-aware methods on SMAP/MSL) | Most theoretically promising of the five (directly targets a structural blind spot of the current design), but would be a novel contribution, not a literature replication |
| 3. Adaptive Q/R | High — innovation-correlation / Sage-Husa / VB-AKF are all genuinely online and closed-form-adjacent | Some (AUV navigation anomaly flagging, aircraft sensor fault diagnosis via recursive R estimation) | Partial (AUV, aircraft — not spacecraft) | Likely improves tracking through regime drift; unproven whether it improves detection F-score specifically; needs fault-gating to avoid contamination teaching the filter to ignore real anomalies |
| 4. Switching/IMM | Medium — more moving parts (mode bank, transition probabilities, mixing) than 1 or 3 | Strong for fault-mode diagnosis (Tudoroiu & Khorasani 2007, Rapoport & Oshman 2007); one 2024 paper (Maleki Sadr & Qaraqe) frames IMM-style fusion as telemetry anomaly detection with strong reported gains | Yes for the 2024 paper (SMAP, MSL rover) but full method detail unretrieved; older IMM-fault papers are simulation-only or lab-satellite | Possible, but the found framing is fault-mode-hypothesis banks, not operational-regime banks — would require adapting the paradigm rather than replicating it |
| 5. EKF/UKF (nonlinear) | High implementation cost relative to payoff — sigma-point/linearization machinery for channels with no known nonlinear dynamics | Strong but concentrated in attitude/orbital-dynamics fault detection, where nonlinearity is physically motivated | Yes for attitude/star-sensor case (on-orbit validated) — but not for generic housekeeping channels | Least likely of the five; a paper on directly comparable SMAP/MSL data explicitly says EKF/UKF "often compromise accuracy" relative to alternatives |

**Bottom line for the paper's future-work section:** the multivariate/correlated variant (#2) is the most scientifically interesting direction — it is the only one that targets a documented, named failure mode (cross-channel-correlation-breaking anomalies) that the current independent-per-channel design is structurally blind to, and non-Kalman work on the same data class (SMAP/MSL) confirms correlation-awareness measurably helps — but it would be presented honestly as an open gap / novel extension rather than a replication of established Kalman-specific anomaly-detection prior art, since no such prior art was found. The adaptive (#3) and robust (#1) variants are the safest "few-hours" bets with the most directly applicable lightweight algorithms, but their documented benefits are in state-tracking-accuracy terms (not anomaly-detection F-score terms) and in non-spacecraft domains, so claims of improvement over the existing filter would need to be empirically demonstrated rather than cited. The switching/IMM variant (#4) has the strongest spacecraft-specific pedigree but for a different problem framing (fault-hypothesis banks, not regime detection) and is the most implementation-heavy of the tractable options. The nonlinear EKF/UKF variant (#5) has the weakest case for this specific data (generic housekeeping channels, no stated nonlinear physical dynamics) and should likely be deprioritized or mentioned only briefly in future work.
