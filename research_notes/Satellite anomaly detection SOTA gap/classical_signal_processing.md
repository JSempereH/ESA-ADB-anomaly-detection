# Classical Signal-Processing Approaches as Generic Multivariate Anomaly Detectors (2024-2026 evidence)

Scope note: this covers wavelet/multi-resolution, Fourier/spectral-residual, and Kalman-filter/state-space methods for GENERIC multivariate industrial/telemetry anomaly detection, explicitly excluding satellite orbit/attitude/GNC Kalman filtering applications.

## Wavelets: what do FreqWave-TranDuD and FreFilterTST report, and is there other 2024-2026 wavelet work worth citing?

### Takeaway
FreqWave-TranDuD is a real, verifiable 2025 IEEE Access paper, but it is a **deep learning model that uses wavelet/Fourier transforms as feature-extraction front-ends inside an encoder-decoder + LSTM**, not a classical standalone wavelet detector, and its claimed evaluation is on public benchmark-style datasets (not confirmed real industrial/spacecraft telemetry beyond generic domain labels). "FreFilterTST" (Scientific Reports 2026, frequency-domain restoration + graph sparsification) could not be located or verified under that name in any index (arXiv, OpenAlex, Scientific Reports search, Google-style web search) — treat it as unconfirmed/possibly misremembered until a DOI is produced. Wavelets in the current literature overwhelmingly appear as a feature/preprocessing component bolted onto deep architectures, not as a competitive standalone classical detector.

### Cited Findings
- FreqWave-TranDuD ("FreqWave-TranDuD: A Multivariate Time Series Anomaly Detection Method Based on Wavelet and Fourier Transforms"), IEEE Access, 2025, DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2025.3557571, first author Yashi Huang (Ocean University of China) — confirmed to exist via OpenAlex metadata. — [OpenAlex record](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109%2FACCESS.2025.3557571)
- The method combines FFT (captures global periodic patterns), Discrete Wavelet Transform (captures local patterns), and an LSTM (temporal dependencies); extracted features are concatenated and fed into an encoder-decoder for anomaly scoring — i.e., it is a hybrid deep model, not a closed-form classical detector. — [OpenAlex record](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109%2FACCESS.2025.3557571)
- The paper claims evaluation on "six public datasets" spanning domains described only as "aerospace and water treatment" (consistent with SMAP/MSL-style and SWaT-style benchmarks), with claims of outperforming unspecified "advanced baseline models." No dataset names, baseline names, or numeric F1/precision/recall figures were retrievable from available metadata/abstract indexes — full text behind the DOI was not accessible to this research pass. — [OpenAlex record](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109%2FACCESS.2025.3557571)
- No source (arXiv, OpenAlex, direct web search, or Scientific Reports site search) returned any record for a paper titled "FreFilterTST" in Scientific Reports 2026 or any other venue, under searches combining "frequency domain restoration," "graph sparsification," and "anomaly detection." — [Search: FreFilterTST](https://www.google.com) (no result)
- A related but distinct 2022 paper exists: "Multiscale Wavelet Graph AutoEncoder for Multivariate Time-Series Anomaly Detection," IEEE Transactions on Instrumentation and Measurement (DOI 10.1109/TIM.2022.3223142) — again a deep graph-autoencoder architecture using wavelet multiscale decomposition as a feature layer, not a classical standalone method; full text/results were not retrieved in this pass. — [OpenAlex record](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109%2FTIM.2022.3223142)
- A 2025 IEEE ISCAS conference paper, "TWavefussion: Wavelet-based Diffusion with Transformer for Multivariate Time Series Anomaly Detection," DOI via OpenAlex (10.1109/ISCAS56072.2025.11043617), again pairs wavelets with a diffusion+transformer deep architecture rather than using wavelets alone. — [OpenAlex record](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109%2FISCAS56072.2025.11043617)

- Older/adjacent classical (non-deep) wavelet work found: wavelet-energy-based sensor validation/fault classification (foundational, pre-2020, validated on simulations and a pressure transducer) and continuous-wavelet-transform scalogram-based sensor validation for energy plants (IJPHM) — these are single-sensor validation methods, not generic multivariate anomaly detectors, and predate the 2024-2026 window. — [IJPHM scalogram paper](https://papers.phmsociety.org/index.php/ijphm/article/view/2670)
- A 2024 Machines-journal paper (expanded from a 2023 IEEE SSCI conference paper) applies discrete wavelet transforms on microcontrollers for embedded/IoT sensor fault isolation in real time, reporting high detection rate with low false alarms — but this is an embedded single/few-sensor fault-isolation setting, not a generic multivariate benchmark comparison against deep SOTA. — [Preprint](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202408.1710)

### Inferences
- The 2024-2026 wavelet literature trend is clearly toward using wavelets as a **frequency-domain feature-extraction component embedded inside deep architectures** (encoder-decoders, graph autoencoders, diffusion+transformer models), rather than as a standalone, lightweight, closed-form classical detector. This means wavelets are not being validated as a *generic competitive alternative* to deep learning — they are being absorbed into deep learning as a preprocessing trick, which does not answer the question of whether classical/cheap wavelet methods alone are competitive.
- None of the identified 2024-2026 wavelet papers were confirmed to use real industrial/spacecraft housekeeping telemetry; the datasets referenced (by domain description, "aerospace," "water treatment") strongly suggest the standard academic benchmarks (SMAP/MSL, SWaT) rather than new real-world telemetry collections.

## Fourier/spectral-residual: current best evidence for SR-style anomaly detection in 2024-2026 multivariate settings, and does it handle cross-channel structure?

### Takeaway
The spectral-residual (SR) lineage from Microsoft's SR-CNN (2019, arXiv:1906.03821) remains fundamentally a **univariate, per-channel** method in its original and production (ML.NET) forms, and no 2024-2026 paper was found that extends SR-CNN itself to natively model cross-channel/multivariate structure — recent "frequency-domain" multivariate anomaly/forecasting work (e.g., FilterNet, FilterTS) instead replaces the classical SR algorithm entirely with learnable frequency-filtering layers inside deep networks, rather than extending SR's closed-form recipe.

### Cited Findings
- SR-CNN's spectral residual step operates on a single sequence x: (1) FFT to get the log amplitude spectrum, (2) compute the spectral residual, (3) inverse FFT back to the spatial domain; hyperparameters are just window size, estimated points, and a detection threshold — a per-series univariate pipeline. — [arXiv:1906.03821](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.03821)
- Microsoft's production implementation (`SrCnnAnomalyEstimator` in ML.NET) takes "an input column of a single value type," confirming the univariate design persists in the deployed/production form. — [ML.NET docs](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-nz/dotnet/api/microsoft.ml.transforms.timeseries.srcnnanomalyestimator)
- A 2024 CRAN R package (`spectralAnomaly`, v0.1.1, Sept 2024) implements the spectral residual algorithm as a single-series tool, explicitly built on the original Ren et al. 2019 Microsoft paper — confirming no multivariate extension has entered even newer software implementations as of 2024. — [CRAN record](https://packages.oit.ncsu.edu/cran/web/packages/spectralAnomaly/index.html)
- No 2024-2026 paper extending SR-CNN itself to a genuinely cross-channel/multivariate spectral-residual formulation was found; search results instead surfaced newer, unrelated deep frequency-filtering forecasting architectures (FilterNet/arXiv:2411.01623, FilterTS/arXiv:2505.04158) that use learnable frequency filters inside transformer/MLP backbones rather than the classical SR recipe. — [FilterNet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.01623), [FilterTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.04158v1)
- Graph-spectral anomaly detection (different lineage from SR-CNN, but also "spectral" in the graph-Laplacian sense) does address cross-channel/node structure: e.g., "Community-based anomaly detection using spectral graph filtering" treats anomalies as high-frequency components with respect to a graph Fourier basis built from community/correlation structure across channels/nodes, and "High-Pass Graph Convolutional Network for Enhanced Anomaly Detection" (arXiv:2411.01817, 2024) extends this to a GCN. These are "multivariate-aware" in the sense of modeling channel relationships via a graph, but they are graph-spectral, not classical time-domain SR-CNN, and are themselves deep/learned architectures (GCN). — [arXiv:2201.09936](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.09936), [arXiv:2411.01817](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.01817)

### Inferences
- The SR-CNN lineage has essentially not been advanced as a classical multivariate method in 2024-2026; its natural per-channel/univariate limitation means any multivariate use requires either (a) running it independently per channel and separately fusing/aggregating channel-level anomaly scores (an approach consistent with a k-sigma-style per-channel baseline design), or (b) abandoning the closed-form SR recipe for a learned frequency-filter deep network. There is no evidence of a lightweight, closed-form, genuinely cross-channel spectral-residual detector in the recent literature.
- For a benchmark context like ESA-ADB's, an SR-style method would most naturally slot in as another per-channel baseline (akin to GlobalSTD), not as a method claimed to capture multivariate/cross-channel structure.

### Gaps
- No evidence was found either way about SR-style methods specifically applied to real spacecraft/industrial telemetry in 2024-2026 (as opposed to the original 2019 Microsoft cloud-service-metrics use case). This is a gap: SR-CNN's original validation was on Microsoft's own cloud monitoring data, not industrial/spacecraft telemetry, and no 2024-2026 follow-up on telemetry-specific data was located.
- Could not confirm whether any 2024-2026 paper directly pits classical per-channel SR against modern multivariate deep contamination-robust baselines (autoencoders, contrastive methods) in a head-to-head multivariate benchmark; this comparison appears essentially absent from the recent indexed literature.

## Kalman filters / state-space models: best recent (2024-2026) generic multivariate work; what do KalmanAE and BSSAD report and are they online-capable?

### Takeaway
BSSAD (arXiv:2301.13031, Jan 2023 — note: pre-2024, flagged per the research constraints as evidence the direction may be under-explored recently) is a real, verifiable hybrid Bayesian state-space + neural-network framework reporting F1 > 0.95 across five unnamed datasets, but it explicitly hybridizes particle filters/ensemble Kalman filters WITH trained recurrent-network/autoencoder components — it is not a pure closed-form, gradient-free online detector. "KalmanAE" (IEEE TIM 2023, "Deep Embedding Optimized Kalman Filter") could not be located or verified under that name through any available search tool; the closest genuine matches are a WPI dissertation on an "Autoencoder-Kalman Filter (AEKF)" (2020, not TIM, not confirmed as an anomaly-detection paper) and unrelated Kalman-filter TIM papers. Across the whole search, no 2024-2026 paper was found applying a pure closed-form (no-training, incrementally-updated) Kalman/state-space model as a standalone generic multivariate industrial-telemetry anomaly detector benchmarked against modern deep baselines.

### Cited Findings
- BSSAD ("BSSAD: Towards A Novel Bayesian State-Space Approach for Anomaly Detection in Multivariate Time Series"), arXiv:2301.13031, Anjum/Lin/Zhan (Univ. of Cincinnati / Univ. of Arkansas), posted 30 Jan 2023 — confirmed via arXiv abstract page. — [arXiv:2301.13031](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13031)
- BSSAD is explicitly a two-module hybrid: a neural network module (RNNs/autoencoders for understanding relationships in the data) plus a Bayesian state-space module (particle filters and ensemble Kalman filters for next-state prediction) — i.e., the state-space component is paired with, not a substitute for, trained deep components. The design is stated as modular (parameters/algorithms swappable), which implies retraining/tuning is part of the pipeline rather than a zero-training online filter. — [arXiv:2301.13031](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13031)
- BSSAD reports F1-score greater than 0.95 across five (unnamed in retrievable abstract/metadata) datasets, and proposes using Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) as a more complete accuracy metric; specific per-dataset numbers, baseline names, and confirmation of whether baselines included modern deep contamination-robust methods were not retrievable (PDF was binary/unparseable via the available fetch tool). — [arXiv:2301.13031](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13031)
- No search (multiple phrasings, including exact-title search) returned any record of a paper titled "KalmanAE" or "Deep Embedding Optimized Kalman Filter" in IEEE Transactions on Instrumentation and Measurement or elsewhere. This should be flagged to the requester as unverifiable/possibly misremembered. — [Search results across multiple queries, no match found]
- Closest related (but non-matching, non-confirmed) work: a WPI PhD dissertation, "Robust State Estimation and Deep Learning for Time Series Analysis," describes an "Autoencoder-Kalman Filter (AEKF)" where a neural network learns point-wise measurement-noise covariance matrices (replacing a single fixed covariance), intended to help the filter down-weight outlier measurements — this is conceptually adjacent to "KalmanAE" but is a 2020-era WPI dissertation, not a 2023 IEEE TIM paper, and was not confirmed to be framed as an anomaly-detection benchmark paper. — [WPI repository](https://digital.wpi.edu/show/7p88ck07w)
- A 2024 arXiv paper, "Model-Free Unsupervised Anomaly Detection Framework in Multivariate Time-Series of Industrial Dynamical Systems" (arXiv:2405.08349), explicitly positions itself in the "conventional approaches" category (vs. deep learning), citing reduced training-data needs, explainability, and incremental/online learning with operator feedback as its motivating advantages — but its abstract does not mention Kalman filters or an explicit state-space formulation ("model-free" appears to mean it avoids an explicit system model), and dataset/baseline details could not be retrieved from the abstract. This is relevant context (the "lightweight/online/explainable" value proposition the user is probing for Kalman-style methods) but is NOT itself confirmed to be a Kalman-filter method. — [arXiv:2405.08349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.08349)
- Field Kalman Filter (FKF) work for multimode industrial process anomaly detection (differentiating anomalies from normal-mode switches), validated on simulated multimode process and pilot-scale multiphase flow data — found via Imperial College record, but appears to be circa-2021 work, i.e., pre-2024. — [Imperial Spiral record](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/entities/publication/a2a4484e-d9d1-45d1-8459-7bcb2cbdf952)
- Standard residual-based Kalman-filter detection (predict via KF, chi-squared test on innovation/residual vs. threshold) is described as the baseline approach in industrial control system attack-detection literature (arXiv:2106.07980, 2021) — this confirms the "closed-form, residual-normalized-by-predictive-variance" recipe the user is asking about is a well-established pattern in the ICS/process-monitoring literature, but this specific source predates the 2024-2026 window. — [arXiv:2106.07980](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.07980)
- No 2024-2026 paper specifically applying an Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF) to generic multivariate industrial sensor anomaly detection was found; the search surfaced only older (2021-2023) adjacent Kalman-filter process-monitoring papers and a TUM master's thesis in progress (2025, results unavailable) on "sensor-fusion based Kalman filter" anomaly detection. — [Search: UKF industrial anomaly detection 2024 2025, no clear 2024-2026 match found]

### Inferences
- Despite extensive searching, **no 2024-2026 paper was found presenting a pure, closed-form, gradient-free, online/incremental Kalman or state-space detector as a standalone generic multivariate telemetry anomaly method benchmarked against modern deep baselines.** The pattern across the literature (BSSAD, AEKF, FKF, model-free "conventional" framework) is that whenever Kalman/state-space ideas appear in anomaly detection work, they are either (a) hybridized with trained neural components, or (b) from 2020-2023, pre-dating the requested window. ## Is there a published comparison showing classical spectral/state-space methods beating or matching modern deep methods specifically on REAL telemetry (not synthetic/academic benchmarks)?

### Takeaway
The strongest and most directly relevant evidence is NOT spectral/state-space-specific but a broader classical-vs-deep comparison literature showing **no consistent deep-learning advantage** in multivariate time-series anomaly detection generally — notably Rewicki et al. (Applied Sciences, 2023) concluding classical methods generally outperform deep methods, and "Do Deep Neural Networks Contribute to Multivariate Time Series Anomaly Detection?" (arXiv:2204.01637) concluding no family of methods (conventional/ML/deep) consistently outperforms the others across five real-world datasets. However, both predate or sit at the edge of the 2024-2026 window, and neither is specifically about wavelet/spectral-residual/Kalman methods, nor confirmed to use real industrial/spacecraft telemetry rather than the standard public benchmarks (SMD/SWaT/PSM/SMAP/MSL-style).

### Cited Findings
- Rewicki, Denzler, and Niebling, "Is it worth it? Comparing six deep and classical methods for unsupervised anomaly detection in time series," Applied Sciences 2023 (arXiv:2212.11080) — compares three classical and three deep unsupervised methods; stated conclusion: classical machine learning methods generally outperform deep learning methods across a range of anomaly types, with the paper also assessing how well each method incorporates prior knowledge about anomalies. Full-text numeric tables and exact dataset identities (real telemetry vs. academic benchmark) could not be extracted (binary PDF, not parseable by available tooling). — [arXiv:2212.11080](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.11080)
- "Do Deep Neural Networks Contribute to Multivariate Time Series Anomaly Detection?" (arXiv:2204.01637, 2022) compares sixteen conventional/ML/deep methods on five real-world open datasets; conclusion stated as "no family of methods outperforms the others," urging benchmarks to include all three categories rather than assuming deep learning superiority. Dataset names and numeric tables were not retrievable from the abstract-level fetch. — [arXiv:2204.01637](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.01637)
- Wu and Keogh, "Current Time Series Anomaly Detection Benchmarks are Flawed and are Creating the Illusion of Progress" (2020 preprint, arXiv:2009.13807) — foundational critique arguing most public AD benchmark datasets suffer systematic flaws (triviality, mislabeling, run-to-failure bias, etc.), meaning much of the apparent deep-learning progress on these benchmarks may be illusory; introduces the UCR Time Series Anomaly Archive as a more rigorous replacement. Pre-dates the 2024-2026 window but is foundational to interpreting all later benchmark claims (including FreqWave-TranDuD's and BSSAD's). — [arXiv:2009.13807](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.13807v1)
- PATH (arXiv:2411.13951, Correia/Goos/Bäck/Kononova, Big Data Research 2025) — a new 2024/2025 benchmark built from a real automotive electric-powertrain Simulink simulation (not a purely synthetic/academic SMD-style set, though also not real deployed-fleet telemetry), explicitly created because "existing public datasets are too small, not diverse, and feature trivial anomalies." The paper compares classical and deep learning anomaly detectors on realistic drive-cycle data and notes that the unsupervised-threshold-calibration step is often the weakest link, with performance dropping sharply when training data is contaminated — specific classical-vs-deep numeric results were not retrieved in this pass. — [arXiv:2411.13951](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.13951)
- A 2024 arXiv paper (arXiv:2405.08349) frames "conventional" (non-deep) feature-engineered methods as advantageous specifically for real industrial dynamical systems because of reduced training-data requirements, explainability, and incremental/online learning — consistent with, but not identical to, a classical-signal-processing argument; evaluated only on "two examples," not confirmed as large-scale real telemetry. — [arXiv:2405.08349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.08349)
- ESA-ADB itself (Kotowski et al., arXiv:2406.17826) — the benchmark the requester's own project is built on — confirms that its published baseline suite is PCC, HBOS, isolation forest, KNN, global-std-threshold (STD3/STD5), DC-VAE-ESA, and Telemanom/pruned-Telemanom variants. Notably, **no wavelet, spectral-residual, or Kalman-filter/state-space baseline appears in the published ESA-ADB baseline set**, on real multi-mission ESA spacecraft telemetry. — [arXiv:2406.17826](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.17826)

### Inferences
- Taken together, the classical-vs-deep comparison literature (Rewicki 2023, arXiv:2204.01637, Wu & Keogh 2020) consistently undercuts the assumption that deep learning is a safe default for multivariate anomaly detection, which supports including classical/cheap baselines in any serious benchmark. However, this literature is about classical ML (e.g., PCA, isolation forest, simple statistical thresholds) in general — it does NOT specifically validate wavelet, spectral-residual, or Kalman-filter methods as the classical representatives, and none of it is confirmed to use real industrial/spacecraft telemetry (as opposed to standard academic benchmark datasets, several of which Wu & Keogh argue are flawed).
- The absence of any spectral/state-space baseline in ESA-ADB's own published baseline suite (PCC/HBOS/iForest/KNN/STD/DC-VAE/Telemanom) is itself evidence that this is an open gap in the specific benchmark the user's project extends — directly relevant to the final question below.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that specifically benchmarks wavelet, spectral-residual, or Kalman/state-space methods against deep contamination-robust methods (autoencoders, contrastive learning) on confirmed REAL (not synthetic/academic) industrial or spacecraft telemetry in 2024-2026. This is a genuine, notable gap in the literature as searched.

## Would a per-channel local-level/local-trend Kalman filter be a well-grounded but well-trodden addition to a benchmark that already has GlobalSTD (k-sigma) and a closed-form multivariate ridge forecaster, or would it add genuinely new information?

### Takeaway
It would be a **well-grounded, cheap, and defensible addition that adds genuinely new information to this specific benchmark** (ESA-ADB and similar), because (a) no published ESA-ADB baseline set includes a Kalman/state-space method, (b) the broader 2024-2026 literature shows essentially no one has published a pure closed-form per-channel Kalman/local-level-model baseline evaluated against modern deep methods on real telemetry, and (c) the underlying technique (local-level model / dynamic linear model, Harvey-style structural time series, closed-form recursive Kalman update, residual normalized by predictive variance) is old, well-understood statistical theory — meaning there is low risk of methodological novelty rejection, but genuine value in being among the few to actually report it against modern deep baselines on real telemetry.

### Cited Findings
- ESA-ADB's own published baseline suite (PCC, HBOS, isolation forest, KNN, STD3/STD5 global-std threshold, DC-VAE-ESA, Telemanom/pruned-Telemanom) contains a per-channel k-sigma method (STD3/STD5, i.e., GlobalSTD) but no Kalman filter, local-level model, or any other state-space baseline. — [arXiv:2406.17826](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.17826)
- The "local level model" is textbook-described as the simplest structural time series model: an observed series as a stochastically evolving level plus independent measurement noise, with a signal-to-noise ratio defined as the ratio of level-disturbance variance to observation-noise variance — this is exactly the closed-form, two-parameter recursive filter the user describes, confirming it is a known, standard, decades-old statistical construct (Harvey 1989 structural time series framework), not a novel architecture. — [MetricGate docs](https://metricgate.com/docs/local-level-model/)
- Standard residual-based Kalman-filter fault/anomaly detection (predict via KF, chi-squared/normalized-residual test against threshold) is explicitly described as the established baseline approach in industrial control system attack-detection literature — confirming the "residual normalized by the filter's own predictive variance" design is the standard, well-trodden recipe, not something requiring new theoretical justification. — [arXiv:2106.07980](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.07980)
- Despite being well-established theory, no 2024-2026 paper was found that actually reports this exact recipe (per-channel local-level/local-trend KF, closed-form, online) evaluated head-to-head against modern deep contamination-robust multivariate anomaly detectors on real industrial or spacecraft telemetry — i.e., the theory is old and solid, but the specific empirical comparison the user is proposing to run appears to not yet exist in the citable literature.
- A 2016 patent (US20160062950A1) already describes "systems and methods for anomaly detection and guided analysis using structural time-series models" — flagging an expected value range per point from a structural time-series model and detecting anomalies outside that range — confirming the core idea has long been recognized as practically useful (enough to patent) but reinforcing that it is not a novel research contribution in itself. — [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20160062950A1/en)

### Inferences
- Because (a) the statistical technique itself is old and well-understood (no risk of it being "wrong" or contentious), (b) it is NOT currently in the ESA-ADB baseline set or in any found 2024-2026 benchmark paper evaluated against modern deep methods on real telemetry, and (c) the broader classical-vs-deep literature (Rewicki 2023, arXiv:2204.01637) suggests classical statistical baselines are empirically competitive in general, adding a per-channel local-level/local-trend Kalman filter is best framed as: "a well-grounded, low-novelty-risk classical baseline that happens to be absent from the specific benchmark literature the user is extending" — i.e., it is simultaneously well-trodden (as theory/practice in SPC and control engineering) and genuinely new information (as an empirically reported comparison point in this specific modern multivariate-telemetry-benchmark context). This is a legitimate and useful thing to add, not a redundant rehash of GlobalSTD or the ridge forecaster: a local-level/local-trend KF differs from GlobalSTD by having an adaptive, time-varying expected level/trend (vs. GlobalSTD's fixed global statistics) and differs from a ridge forecaster by being per-channel/univariate-dynamics with a principled, closed-form predictive-variance-normalized residual (vs. the ridge model's cross-channel linear structure) — so it fills a distinct point in the baseline design space (univariate adaptive dynamics) that neither existing baseline covers.
- Expect such a per-channel KF baseline to behave similarly in spirit to an adaptive/exponentially-weighted version of GlobalSTD — likely to catch slow drifts and trend-breaks that a fixed global-std threshold misses, but, like GlobalSTD, to miss cross-channel/contextual anomalies that only manifest as relationship changes between channels (which the ridge forecaster or deep multivariate methods are better positioned to catch). This is an inference, not a cited result, since no direct empirical comparison was found.

### Gaps
- No empirical result (2024-2026 or earlier) was found quantifying how a per-channel local-level/local-trend Kalman filter actually performs against GlobalSTD, ridge forecasters, or deep multivariate baselines specifically on spacecraft or generic industrial telemetry — so the expected magnitude of any improvement (or lack thereof) cannot be cited and would need to be established empirically by the user's own benchmark run.
- No evidence was found on whether such a baseline has been tried and found NOT worth reporting (i.e., a possible file-drawer effect) — absence of evidence is not evidence of absence here.
