# Prior-work gap check: walk-forward online + fully unsupervised L2 + ESA-ADB Mission 2 anomalies-only

## Does any paper citing ESA-ADB (arXiv:2406.17826) use a walk-forward/online protocol and report anomalies-only results?

### Takeaway
One paper — arXiv:2609.10017, "Structure-Aware Unsupervised Anomaly Detection for Spacecraft Telemetry with Adaptive EVT Thresholding" (Sept 2026) — comes extremely close: it is genuinely walk-forward/online (monthly incremental retraining across the whole mission), fully label-free in both training and threshold calibration, and evaluated on real ESA-AD Mission 1 and Mission 2. **This paper is co-authored by Javier Sempere** (the same name as this repository's git user / this project's owner), so it should be treated as the user's own existing/parallel work, not an independent third party. Critically, even this paper does **not** report F0.5 broken out for the "anomaly" category alone vs. "rare nominal event" category — it only reports an aggregate event-wise F0.5 per mission (M1=0.700, M2=0.698), and it does not mention or engage with ESA-ADB's own Supplementary Table 9 near-zero (≤0.001) Mission-2-full-channel-anomalies-only result at all. All other citing papers found use fixed train/test splits or protocols that could not be confirmed as genuinely walk-forward.

### Cited Findings
- ESA-ADB (arXiv:2406.17826, Kotowski et al. 2024) has 31 citing papers as of this search, per Semantic Scholar's citation graph. — [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:2406.17826/citations)
- **arXiv:2609.10017** ("Structure-Aware Unsupervised Anomaly Detection for Spacecraft Telemetry with Adaptive EVT Thresholding", submitted 9 Sep 2026; authors: Óscar Alcarria, Rafael Sánchez, **Javier Sempere**, Pablo Torrijos, Juan C. Alfaro, Juan M. Auñón, José A. Gámez, José M. Puerta): abstract states the framework "needs no labels, prior fault knowledge, or mission-specific tuning," combines "incremental monthly retraining, statistical model selection, and adaptive Extreme Value Theory (EVT) thresholding," and reports F0.5 = 0.700 (Mission 1) and F0.5 = 0.698 (Mission 2) "under chronological evaluation" on the ESA Anomalies Dataset. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10017)
- The paper's protocol is explicitly walk-forward across the full mission: "the model is trained exclusively on the previous month's data and evaluated on the subsequent month," with a reported cost-analysis count of 161 monthly windows for Mission 1 and 40 for Mission 2 (i.e., essentially one retrain-and-evaluate fold per month across the entire mission history). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10017v1)
- Training is label-free: the only pre-training filter named is MAD-based (statistical) sample filtering; the paper describes the framework as operating "without labels." The paper does not state that training windows are purged of contamination using ground truth, consistent with a fully unsupervised (contaminated) training condition. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10017v1)
- Threshold calibration is explicitly label-free: "All hyperparameters were fixed a priori without label-based optimization" (q=0.98, α=0.01, N_min=30, λ=δ=0.05), and the paper notes α "is not a guaranteed false-positive rate." This is an EVT-quantile-based calibration mechanism, **not** an "alarm budget"/max-alarms-per-period style constraint — no such budget framing appears in the paper. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10017v1)
- The paper reports only aggregate event-wise metrics in its results table; although Figures 3–4 have series labeled "Anom.", "Rare", and "Com. G." (presumably per-category breakdowns), **no separate F0.5 numbers for the anomaly category alone are quoted in the text**, for either mission. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10017v1)
- The paper cites ESA-ADB's own static 50/50-split baselines (best all-channel F0.5 of 0.061 for M1 and 0.241 for M2) as the comparison point, but does **not** cite or discuss ESA-ADB's Supplementary Table 9 or the specific near-zero (≤0.001) Mission-2-full-channel-anomalies-only result. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10017v1)
- The paper does not mention or compare against arXiv:2607.07335 ("Toward Deployable Satellite Anomaly Detection") anywhere in its text or references. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10017v1)

## Characterization of arXiv:2607.07335 ("Toward Deployable Satellite Anomaly Detection: A Benchmark Study on Large-Scale ESA-ADB Telemetry")

### Takeaway
The user's prior characterization is confirmed and extracted in more detail: the paper uses fixed (non-walk-forward) chronological splits for both its unsupervised and supervised arms, calibrates unsupervised thresholds via a 99.9th-percentile-of-validation-scores rule (not label-based, not an alarm-budget), reports ECOD F0.5 = 0.4484 on Mission 1's lightweight channel subset (matching the "~0.448" figure cited), and never mentions or implements any online/incremental/walk-forward variant anywhere in the paper. Results are not broken down by event category (anomalies vs. rare nominal events) anywhere in the paper.

### Cited Findings
- Authors: Andrea Nguyen, Dafne Rozenberg, Yeying Zhu, and Peng Hu; submitted 8 July 2026 (arXiv:2607.07335, cs.CE). — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07335)
- Dataset/protocol: ESA-ADB Mission 1 (84 months) and Mission 2 (21 months); lightweight subsets use Mission 1 channels 41–46 and Mission 2 channels 18–28; full channel sets were also tested for both missions. For unsupervised models, "the first half of the series is used for training and validation, and the second half for testing," with "the final three months of the training portion" used as validation — a fixed chronological split, not walk-forward. Supervised models use predefined fixed training-window lengths (M1: 3/10/21/42/84 months; M2: 1/5/10/21 months), and the paper does not mention any online, incremental, or walk-forward training variant anywhere. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07335v1)
- Threshold calibration: Elliptic Envelope and ECOD both use a threshold set at the 99.9th percentile of validation-set anomaly scores (EE additionally uses contamination=0.001, support_fraction=0.9); no explicit threshold-calibration method is stated for the supervised models. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07335v1)
- Metrics: event-based precision, recall, F0.5, event-based accuracy, and PR-AUC; no point-wise metrics reported; an event counts as correct if it overlaps a ground-truth interval. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07335v1)
- ECOD F0.5 by table: Mission 1 lightweight = 0.4484 (Table II); Mission 1 full = 0.065 (Table III); Mission 2 lightweight = 0.2187 (Table IV); Mission 2 full = 0.0619 (Table V). None of these are category-filtered (anomalies-only); labels are multi-class but "results are not broken down by category," and it is unclear how rare-event/system-condition classes are mapped for the binary-label unsupervised models. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07335v1)
- Two possible data-quality issues were flagged by the fetch: the Mission 2 lightweight GCN row (Table IV) appears identical to the Mission 1 full-set GCN row (Table III) — possibly a copy/paste error in the paper; and the text claims ~9 hours/epoch for Multiscale CNN on Mission 1 while Table VI lists ~0.5 hours/epoch — an internal inconsistency. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07335v1)

### Inferences
- This paper is a pure fixed-split benchmark study; it provides no walk-forward/online point of comparison at all, so it is not a match for the proposed study's protocol, and it also does not isolate anomalies-only performance, so it is not a match for the proposed study's category framing either.

### Gaps
- None of substance — this question was answered directly from primary-source full text.

## Characterization of arXiv:2506.01849 ("Trojan Horse Hunt in Time Series Forecasting for Space Operations")

### Takeaway
This is a Kaggle-style competition paper about detecting backdoor/trojan triggers planted in N-HiTS time-series *forecasting* models for space operations — an AI-security/model-auditing problem, not an anomaly-detection-evaluation study. It is not relevant to the proposed study's research questions about online/unsupervised anomaly detection protocols or event-wise metrics on ESA-ADB.

### Cited Findings
- The paper (submitted June 2025) describes a competition where participants recovered 45 hidden triggers (short multivariate time-series segments) injected into the training data of 45 poisoned N-HiTS forecasting models; participants were given a public telemetry dataset, a clean reference model, and the poisoned models; the baseline method was Neural Cleanse, adapted (imperfectly, per the authors) from image-domain backdoor detection to time series. — [fugumt summary](https://fugumt.com/fugumt/paper_check/2506.01849v1), [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.01849)
- A follow-up paper, arXiv:2603.20108 ("Trojan horse hunt in deep forecasting models: Insights from the European Space Agency competition," prepared for the International Journal of Forecasting), reports over 200 teams participated and covers best solutions and lessons learned. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20108)

### Inferences
- Neither this paper nor its follow-up addresses anomaly-detection performance metrics, walk-forward training protocols, or label-free threshold calibration; it is a model-security/backdoor-auditing competition, fundamentally orthogonal to the proposed study's research question.

### Gaps
