# Online + Fully Unsupervised Multivariate TSAD Under Contaminated Training Data (2024-2026 landscape)

## Is there a comprehensive 2024-2026 survey covering this exact intersection (online training + fully unsupervised + contaminated data)?

### Takeaway
No. The single closest survey — Correia et al., "Online Model-based Anomaly Detection in Multivariate Time Series: Taxonomy, Survey, Research Challenges and Future Directions" (arXiv:2408.03747, published in *Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence* 138(part A), 2024) — covers online + semi-/unsupervised model-based MTS anomaly detection, but its own abstract does not single out "contaminated training data" as a taxonomy axis, and no other 2024-2026 survey was found that explicitly crosses all three dimensions (true streaming parameter updates, zero labels anywhere including thresholding, and an unlabeled-contaminated training stream rather than a clean reference set).

### Cited Findings
- Correia et al.'s survey (arXiv:2408.03747, v3 Sep 2024) is authored by Lucas (Ferreira) Correia, Jan-Christoph Goos, Philipp Klein, Thomas Bäck, and Anna V. Kononova, published in Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence, doi:10.1016/j.engappai.2024.109323 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03747v3)
- Its taxonomy explicitly separates **online vs. offline** and **training vs. inference** as independent axes (i.e., a method can be "offline-trained, online-inference" or vice versa), and it scopes itself to "online semi- and unsupervised" model-based approaches categorized into model families — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03747v3)
- The survey's stated gaps/future directions are about benchmarking quality, not contamination-robustness specifically: it says there is no reliable way to compare approaches, that public datasets have at least one fundamental flaw, that evaluation metrics lack intuitive/representative measures, and that threshold selection in most publications ignores real-world conditions — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03747v3)
- I was unable to retrieve a reliable "cited by" list for this survey (Semantic Scholar returned HTTP 403/429 and OpenAlex returned 404 for the DOI-based lookup I tried), so I cannot confirm which specific 2025-2026 papers position themselves as extending it or what gaps those citing papers identify — this is a genuine gap in my research, not a negative finding.
- A separate, broader 2025 review — "A Review on Self-Supervised Learning in Time Series Anomaly Detection: Recent Advances and Open Challenges" (ACM Computing Surveys, vol. 58, issue 5, doi:10.1145/3770575, authors Sánchez-Ferrera, Calvo, Lozano per search snippet) — exists but its scope is self-supervised learning broadly, not specifically the online+contaminated intersection; I could not confirm from the snippet whether it discusses streaming/contamination at all — [search result](https://ekoizpen-zientifikoa.ehu.eus/documentos/695028cd9244cb45822e8555?lang=en)
- A 2024 Expert Systems With Applications paper (Mejri et al.) argues "a comprehensive and extensive evaluation of recent state-of-the-art techniques taking into account real-world constraints is still needed," extending evaluation beyond precision/recall/F1 to model size and stability — this is itself evidence that the field considers this gap still open as of 2024 — [orbilu.uni.lu PDF](https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/61824/1/1-s2.0-S0957417424017895-main.pdf)

### Inferences
- The absence of a dedicated survey at this exact intersection, combined with Correia et al.'s own admission that benchmarking in the online-unsupervised space is unreliable, suggests this project's walk-forward/contaminated-stream protocol is genuinely addressing an under-served evaluation gap rather than reinventing a solved problem.

## What specific method families or papers (not already covered) does the literature recommend? Are they truly online, and validated on real industrial telemetry?

### Takeaway
Three genuinely new, concrete leads emerged: (1) a drift-aware isolation-forest-ensemble family (IForestASD → EiForestASD → BWOAIF → ADDAEIL, 2021-2025) that is truly incremental (trees are swapped/updated per-drift-signal rather than retrained from scratch) and is a different mechanism from the already-covered Online Isolation Forest (Leveni et al.); (2) a 2025 deep PU-learning method for contaminated-unlabeled anomaly detection (Takahashi et al., arXiv:2405.18929) that is conceptually on-point but not yet applied to time series and not online; (3) an ICML 2024 label-free/online threshold-calibration method (Sun et al., "Online Adaptive Anomaly Thresholding with Confidence Sequences") that is a genuine, non-overlapping contribution to the thresholding half of the problem (distinct from detection method families). None of these three has been validated on real industrial/satellite-type multivariate sensor telemetry beyond what's described below — mostly network/IoT/synthetic streams.

### Cited Findings
- **ADDAEIL** (Li, Nair, Wang; *Algorithms* (MDPI) 18(6):359, June 2025, doi:10.3390/a18060359): an unsupervised framework that pairs a drift detector (statistical distribution test + structural performance assessment of base detectors) with an Isolation-Forest-based ensemble; it swaps out degraded base detectors according to estimated drift intensity while keeping still-useful trees, i.e., genuinely incremental/online updates, not a fixed offline pass. The authors report it "beats existing state-of-the-art methods on both real and synthetic non-stationary streams," though the search snippets did not confirm what "real" data specifically means (not confirmed as industrial/satellite telemetry) — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/083d0cf574b5470180ff9e723d5255f6), [library record](https://library.kab.ac.ug/Record/doaj-art-083d0cf574b5470180ff9e723d5255f6)
- **Predecessor family to ADDAEIL**: IForestASD (extends Isolation Forest to streams via ADWIN/KSWIN drift detectors) and EiForestASD (discards only isolation trees that conflict with a new concept; reports 19% lower computation time and 9% better detection rate than IForestASD) — [MDPI Computers](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-431X/10/1/13), [JECEI PDF](https://jecei.sru.ac.ir/article_2228_5887a393cd9ef17cff8f7873e1791e9a.pdf)
- **BWOAIF** (Bilateral-Weighted Online Adaptive Isolation Forest; Hannák, Horváth, Kádár, Szalai; *Statistical Analysis and Data Mining*, 2023, doi:10.1002/sam.11612): classifies each incoming point and updates its ensemble by discarding old trees and adding new ones, adapting "gradually to slow drift and quickly to sudden distribution changes"; evaluated across shift/split/appearance/disappearance drift types, but the search snippets did not confirm whether real-world (vs. only synthetic) data was used — [Nokia Bell Labs page](https://nokia.com/bell-labs/publications-and-media/publications/bilateral-weighted-online-adaptive-isolation-forest-for-anomaly-detection-in-streaming-data)
- This whole isolation-forest+drift-detector family is mechanistically distinct from the already-covered "Online Isolation Forest" (Leveni et al., ICML 2024) — it is detector-ensemble replacement triggered by explicit drift detection, rather than online tree-structure insertion/update of a single evolving isolation forest — [proceedings.mlr.press/v235/leveni24a](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/leveni24a.html) referenced in search context
- **Deep PU anomaly detection**: Takahashi, Iwata, Kumagai, Yamanaka, "Deep Positive-Unlabeled Anomaly Detection for Contaminated Unlabeled Data" (arXiv:2405.18929, v1 May 2024, v2 Feb 2025). Treats the unlabeled training pool as a mixture of normal and anomalous distributions (explicitly targeting the "labeling every anomaly is hard, so the unlabeled pool is itself contaminated" setting), extending to autoencoders and Deep SVDD. The authors report outperforming existing methods on "various datasets," but their own conclusion states time-series data is left as **future work** — i.e., as of Feb 2025 this method has not been validated on time series at all, only on (apparently) tabular/image-style datasets — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18929)
- A caveat noted in the paper itself: the method assumes labeled anomalies represent the anomaly distribution, and new anomaly types unseen in training may not follow it (per the paper's own footnote) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18929)
- **Online/label-free threshold calibration**: Sun, Sankararaman, Narayanaswamy, "Online Adaptive Anomaly Thresholding with Confidence Sequences" (ICML 2024 / PMLR v235) — targets "adaptive threshold selection robust to distribution shifts, with statistical guarantees on false-positive/false-negative rates without distributional assumptions," optionally warm-started with offline data — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/sun24h.html)
- Classical foundation still cited as the baseline for label-free streaming thresholds: Siffer et al., "Anomaly Detection in Streams with Extreme Value Theory" (KDD 2017) — SPOT/DSPOT, i.e., automatic thresholding using only a risk parameter, no distributional assumptions — [KDD paper PDF](https://www.eecs.yorku.ca/course_archive/2018-19/F/6412/reading/kdd17p1067.pdf)
- A 2025 benchmarking paper (Boggia, Teixeira de Lima, Malaescu, arXiv:2506.20574, submitted June 2025, under review for VLDB 2026) explicitly studies "the impact of anomalous data present during training" and evaluates alternative loss functions to reduce that influence, using iTransformer and other transformer-based models — but these are fixed-window, offline-trained transformer architectures, not online/incremental, and the abstract did not confirm use of real industrial telemetry vs. standard academic benchmarks — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20574)
- A 2024 study, Nkashama et al., "Deep Learning for Network Anomaly Detection under Data Contamination: Evaluating Robustness and Mitigating Performance Degradation" (arXiv:2407.08838, 2025 Springer LNCS chapter), benchmarks six unsupervised deep detectors under a controlled contamination protocol (following Zong et al. 2018's split: contaminated training set + held-out "contamination set"), with repeated runs and critical-difference-diagram ranking, and proposes a constrained-latent-space autoencoder as a more contamination-resistant mitigation — but this is network-intrusion-detection data, not multivariate industrial/satellite sensor telemetry, and the method is offline/batch-trained — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.08838)

### Inferences
- The isolation-forest-drift-ensemble family (IForestASD/EiForestASD/BWOAIF/ADDAEIL) is the most concrete "genuinely online, not already covered" method family found — it directly satisfies the "true incremental/streaming parameter updates" requirement by construction (trees are added/discarded in response to drift, never a single offline fit). It is the strongest actionable candidate for this project's walk-forward protocol among everything found, though its validation against real industrial multivariate telemetry (vs. network/synthetic streams) is unconfirmed from abstracts alone and would need the full papers checked before adoption.
- PU-learning-for-anomaly-detection is real and active (Takahashi et al. 2024/2025 is a clean, recent, directly-on-topic example of the "contaminated unlabeled pool" framing this project cares about) but it is a genuine gap for time series specifically — the authors themselves flag time series as unaddressed future work, confirming the suspected gap rather than closing it.
- Thresholding (SPOT/DSPOT lineage, extended by Sun et al. ICML 2024) is a separate, mature-ish sub-line from detection-method families; it's worth distinguishing "no labels needed for detection" from "no labels needed for threshold calibration" — the latter has more established online-capable tooling than contamination-robust detection itself does.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether ADDAEIL's or BWOAIF's "real" data evaluation includes genuine multivariate industrial/sensor telemetry (vs. network traffic or synthetic streams) — only abstract/metadata-level snippets were retrievable; the MDPI full text (https://www.mdpi.com/1999-4893/18/6/359) and the Nokia/Wiley full text were not fetched.
- Could not confirm whether the isolation-forest-drift-ensemble family has been benchmarked against LOE/CRTF/RoCA/USDR or any of the already-covered contamination-robust deep methods — these two literatures (streaming isolation-forest-ensembles vs. deep contamination-robust TSAD) appear to be disconnected from each other in the search results, which is itself a notable finding: nobody seems to be cross-comparing "classic streaming ensemble" methods against "deep contamination-robust" methods.

## Positive-unlabeled (PU) learning and active-learning-in-the-loop for streaming TSAD: confirm or deny the suspected gap

### Takeaway
Confirmed as a genuine gap for PU learning specifically applied to time series anomaly detection online. Active learning for streaming anomaly detection is less of a gap — there is real, recent (2024-2025) work — but it reintroduces labels (even if sparse/queried), so it does not satisfy this project's "zero labels anywhere" constraint; it's a different problem framing (label-budget-constrained semi-supervised streaming) rather than a solution to fully-unsupervised contaminated streaming.

### Cited Findings
- No paper combining all three of (a) PU-style labeling assumptions, (b) time-series anomaly detection specifically, and (c) an online/streaming update rule was found. The closest matches split into three non-overlapping groups: streaming PU *classification* (not anomaly detection) going back to Li, Yu, Liu, Ng, "Positive Unlabeled Learning for Data Stream Classification" (SDM 2009) and a follow-up OcVFD-based paper; static (non-streaming) PU anomaly detection, e.g. Zhang et al. (IEEE TMM 2019); and PU learning for time-series *classification* (not anomaly detection), e.g. Nguyen/Li/Ng (IJCAI 2011, LCLC method), Zhang et al. (JCST 2023, shapelet-based), González et al. (Information Sciences 2016) — [search summary, multiple sources, see links above in search transcript]
- The 2024/2025 Takahashi et al. PU-anomaly-detection paper (arXiv:2405.18929, discussed above) is the most modern and directly relevant PU-for-anomaly-detection work, but (a) it is not online/streaming — it is trained as a standard deep model — and (b) its own authors list time-series data as future work, not yet done — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18929)
- **Active learning for streaming TSAD is real and recent**: IJCAI 2025 paper (Chen, He, Wu, Zhao, Qiu), "A Novel Sparse Active Online Learning Framework for Fast and Accurate Streaming Anomaly Detection Over Data Streams" (doi:10.24963/ijcai.2025/305, pp. 2740-2748). It combines ℓ1,2-norm sparse online learning for feature selection with CUR-decomposition-based active learning for instance selection, using CUR residuals as anomaly scores to avoid manual thresholds; reports a 14.06% detection-error reduction vs. five SOTA competitors. The paper is explicitly online/real-time and addresses "model update delays," but it uses active learning (i.e., it queries labels for selected instances) — this is NOT fully unsupervised by this project's definition — [IJCAI 2025 proceedings](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/305)
- Boundary-Driven Active Learning for Anomaly Detection in Time Series Data Streams (Xiaohui Zhou et al., ICASSP 2024) — falls just outside the 2024-2026 window's "recent" emphasis but confirms this is an active area; again, active learning implies label queries, not zero-label operation — [search snippet, ICASSP 2024 record]
- Foundational precursor still cited as the benchmark for streaming active learning in anomaly ensembles: Das et al., tree-based ensembles with batch and streaming active learning, journal version in JAIR 80 (2024) 127-172 (originally arXiv 2019) — [search snippet]
- A 2025(?) arXiv preprint "Detecting the Undetectable: Enhancing Unsupervised Time Series Anomaly Detection via Active Learning" was surfaced (arXiv:2607.00720 per search snippet — note this ID format looks anomalous/possibly a search-tool artifact and could not be independently verified) — flagged as unverified, not to be cited as confirmed without checking arxiv.org directly — [search snippet only, unverified]

### Inferences
- The suspected gap is real: nobody has published a true PU-learning method for streaming/online multivariate time-series anomaly detection as of this search. The adjacent pieces exist (PU for static anomaly detection; PU for time-series classification; streaming PU classification) but have not been combined.
- Active-learning-in-the-loop for streaming TSAD is NOT a gap — it's an active 2024-2025 research line (IJCAI 2025, ICASSP 2024) — but it is a different problem than this project's fully-unsupervised constraint, since it assumes some labeling budget/oracle access. If the project's "zero labels anywhere" constraint is firm, active-learning methods should be excluded on principle, not because they don't exist.

### Gaps
- Could not verify the arXiv:2607.00720 "Detecting the Undetectable" paper's existence/details directly (ID looks potentially malformed — 2607 would imply July 2026, which is in the future relative to this research but within the stated 2024-2026 window; needs direct arxiv.org confirmation before citing).
- Did not find the full text of the IJCAI 2025 sparse active online learning paper, only its abstract — could not confirm whether it has been tested on multivariate time series specifically (vs. generic data streams) or on real industrial data.

## Are there 2025-2026 papers that benchmark multiple contamination-robust methods against each other under a SHARED protocol (not self-selected baselines)?

### Takeaway
Partial yes, but not for this project's exact combination. The clearest shared-protocol, cross-method contamination study found (Nkashama et al., arXiv:2407.08838) is in network-intrusion detection, not multivariate industrial/satellite telemetry, and evaluates offline-trained deep detectors, not online ones. A second 2025 paper (Boggia et al., arXiv:2506.20574) systematically studies contamination's effect on transformer-based TSAD models with a shared evaluation setup, but again offline/fixed-window, and did not confirm real industrial data use. I found no 2025-2026 paper that benchmarks LOE, CRTF, RoCA, USDR, MemStream, or Online Isolation Forest against each other under one shared protocol — the general-purpose benchmark infrastructure that exists (TSB-AD) does not include a contamination axis or true online evaluation mode.

### Cited Findings
- **TSB-AD** ("The Elephant in the Room: Towards A Reliable Time-Series Anomaly Detection Benchmark," Liu & Paparrizos, NeurIPS 2024 Datasets & Benchmarks track): a large shared-protocol benchmark (1070 time series, 40 datasets, 40 algorithms: TSB-AD-U with 350 univariate test sequences, TSB-AD-M with 180 multivariate test sequences), promoting VUS-PR as the evaluation metric and dropping point-adjustment to avoid score inflation. Critically, **each sequence is pre-split into a training part containing ONLY normal data and a labeled test part** — this is the opposite of this project's contaminated-training-stream setting, and there is no evidence in the retrieved material of an online/streaming evaluation mode — [neurips.cc poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/97690), [PyPI project page](https://pypi.org/project/TSB-AD/)
- Nkashama et al. (arXiv:2407.08838, 2024 preprint / 2025 Springer LNCS chapter) is the clearest found example of a genuine shared-protocol, multi-method contamination benchmark: fixed contamination-ratio train/test/contamination-set split (per Zong et al. 2018 methodology), repeated runs for variance, and critical-difference-diagram-based ranking across six unsupervised deep detectors — but domain is network intrusion detection, not industrial MTS, and models are offline-trained — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.08838)
- Boggia et al. (arXiv:2506.20574, June 2025, under review for VLDB 2026) explicitly varies anomaly contamination during training and compares alternative loss functions across transformer-based models (iTransformer and others) under one evaluation setup — the closest 2025 candidate for a shared-protocol TSAD-specific contamination study, but offline/fixed-window and dataset/real-data details were not confirmed from the abstract — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20574)
- No source found cross-benchmarks any of LOE, USDR, MemStream, CRTF/CLEANet, "Angel or Devil," RoCA, GDN, Online Isolation Forest, or Anomaly Transformer against each other in one paper under one protocol — all searches for this combination returned either the original papers themselves (self-benchmarked) or unrelated benchmark infrastructure (TSB-AD) that doesn't include the contamination axis.
- Context critique relevant to why such comparisons are rare: Wu & Keogh, "Current time series anomaly detection benchmarks are flawed and are creating the illusion of progress" — a frequently-cited critique (title only retrieved, not full argument) that is often invoked to explain why standard benchmark comparisons across TSAD papers are considered unreliable in the first place — [search snippet]
- Paparrizos, Boniol, Liu, Palpanas (2025) reportedly review evaluation measures and benchmarks broadly, but this is general TSAD benchmarking methodology, not specifically a contamination-robustness cross-method study — [search snippet, title/authors only, content not fetched]

### Inferences
- This project's finding that LOE/CRTF/RoCA/etc. are each only validated against author-chosen baselines is corroborated by the absence of any independent shared-protocol comparison including them — the one genuine shared-protocol contamination benchmark that does exist (Nkashama et al.) is in an entirely different domain (network security) and doesn't include any of the already-covered methods, so it cannot be used to cross-validate this project's model choices.
- The general TSAD benchmarking community's energy (TSB-AD, VUS-PR, critiques of point-adjustment) has gone into fixing dataset/metric quality for the *standard clean-train/labeled-test* paradigm, not into building a contamination-ratio-controlled, online-capable shared benchmark. That remains an open infrastructure gap as of this research (Oct 2026).

- Did not confirm what domains/datasets Nkashama et al. or Boggia et al. used beyond "network intrusion" and "a diverse set of datasets" respectively — full-text reads of both (arxiv.org/pdf/2407.08838 and arxiv.org/pdf/2506.20574) would clarify real-industrial-data validation status.
